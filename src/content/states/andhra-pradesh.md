## Overview

Andhra Pradesh lies on India's south-east coast, along the Bay of Bengal. It borders Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Telangana, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and the small enclave of Yanam (part of Puducherry) sits on its coast. In its present form, after Telangana was separated in 2014, the state had about 49.6 million people at the 2011 Census and covers roughly 163,000 square kilometres.

The capital is Amaravati, on the Krishna River, and the largest city is the port of Visakhapatnam. Telugu, one of India's classical languages, is the official language. The state is best known to visitors for the hill temple of Tirumala, one of the busiest pilgrimage sites in the world. It also has a long coastline, Buddhist heritage and the classical dance form Kuchipudi.

## History

Greek and early Indian sources describe the Andhras living in the Godavari and Krishna deltas more than two thousand years ago. The Satavahana dynasty ruled much of the Deccan from the 2nd century BCE to the 3rd century CE, from a capital at Dhanyakataka near present-day Amaravati. Buddhism flourished under them, and the Amaravati school of art that developed here influenced art across South and South-East Asia.

Later dynasties included the Andhra Ikshvakus, the Eastern Chalukyas (7th to 12th centuries), the Kakatiyas (12th to early 14th centuries) and the Vijayanagara Empire, which built temples such as Lepakshi. After Vijayanagara's defeat in 1565, the Golconda Sultanate and later the Nizam of Hyderabad controlled parts of the region. By 1801 most of present-day Andhra Pradesh had come under the British Madras Presidency.

After independence, the Telugu-speaking districts of Madras State became **Andhra State** on 1 October 1953, with Kurnool as its capital. On 1 November 1956 it merged with the Telugu-speaking areas of Hyderabad State to form **Andhra Pradesh**, with Hyderabad as the capital. On 2 June 2014 the north-western districts became the separate state of **Telangana**. The Andhra Pradesh government began working from its new capital, Amaravati, in 2017.

## Geography and climate

The state has two broad regions: **Coastal Andhra**, the fertile plains and river deltas along the Bay of Bengal, and **Rayalaseema**, the drier uplands of the south-west. The Eastern Ghats, known locally by names such as the Nallamala, Seshachalam and Papi Hills, separate the coastal plain from the Deccan Plateau. Arma Konda, in Alluri Sitharama Raju district, is the highest peak.

The Godavari and Krishna are the great rivers, both flowing east to wide deltas. The Tungabhadra and Penna are also important. The coastline is about 974 km long.

Most rain falls from July to September in the south-west monsoon. From October to December the north-east monsoon brings more rain, and occasional cyclones, to the coast and the south. Rayalaseema is the driest part of the state.

## Economy

Agriculture and related activities employ most of the population, with rice as the staple crop, and the Krishna–Godavari delta is the main farming region. Fisheries and aquaculture are a major strength. According to Invest India, Andhra Pradesh accounted for 42% of India's marine exports in 2025–26.

Visakhapatnam is the main industrial city and port. The state has reserves of limestone and bauxite and much of India's baryte. The Satish Dhawan Space Centre at Sriharikota is India's main satellite launch site. Invest India lists food processing, textiles, renewable energy, defence manufacturing and medical devices as focus sectors.

## Culture, art and festivals

Telugu has a long literary tradition, and the state is the home of **Kuchipudi**, one of India's classical dance forms, as well as an important centre of Carnatic music. Crafts with Geographical Indication status include Kondapalli toys and Dharmavaram silk saris.

**Sankranti**, the harvest festival in January, is the biggest celebration, and **Ugadi** marks the Telugu New Year. Major Hindu pilgrimage centres include Tirumala, Srisailam, the Kanaka Durga Temple in Vijayawada and Simhachalam near Visakhapatnam.

## Food

Andhra food is known for its heat and tang. Chillies, tamarind, curry leaves and **gongura** (sorrel leaves) are everyday ingredients. A typical meal is rice with *pappu* (dal), *charu* (a thin tamarind soup), vegetable dishes, pickles and *pachadi* (chutneys). **Pulihora** (tamarind rice) is a festival favourite, and Rayalaseema has its own styles of biryani.

## Tourist spots

**Tirumala**, above the city of Tirupati, is the main draw in the south, with **Lepakshi**, **Gandikota** and the **Belum Caves** inland in Rayalaseema. In the north, **Visakhapatnam** has beaches and a major port and is the starting point for the **Araku Valley** and the **Borra Caves**. In the centre, Vijayawada and the new capital region sit on the Krishna near the ancient **Amaravati Stupa**.

## Education and universities

Andhra University in Visakhapatnam and Sri Venkateswara University in Tirupati are the state's best-known public universities. National institutes set up after 2014 include IIT Tirupati and IIM Visakhapatnam.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Visakhapatnam and Vijayawada have the busiest airports, and Tirupati airport serves pilgrims to Tirumala. A new international airport is being built at Bhogapuram, north of Visakhapatnam.

**By rail:** The main Chennai–Kolkata and Chennai–Delhi lines run through the state. Vijayawada is the main railway junction, and Visakhapatnam, Tirupati and Guntur are other major stations.

**By road:** National highways run the length of the coast, linking Chennai with Kolkata through Nellore, Vijayawada and Visakhapatnam. Roads lead inland to Hyderabad and Bengaluru.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Book Tirumala darshan in advance.** Entry to the temple is by timed tickets, queues can be very long, and a dress code applies.
- **Expect spicy food.** Andhra cooking is among the hottest in India, so ask for milder dishes if you need them.
- **Watch the weather on the coast.** October to December is cyclone season in the Bay of Bengal.
- **Carry a light jacket for Araku.** The hills are much cooler than the coast, especially in winter.
