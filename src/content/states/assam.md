## Overview

Assam is a state in north-east India, set in the valleys of the **Brahmaputra** and **Barak** rivers, south of the Eastern Himalaya. It borders Bhutan and Bangladesh, and seven Indian states: Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya and West Bengal. It is linked to the rest of India through the narrow Siliguri Corridor in West Bengal.

Assam had about 31.2 million people at the 2011 Census, the most of any state in the Northeast, and covers 78,438 square kilometres. The capital is **Dispur**, within the Guwahati metropolitan area. Guwahati, the largest city, is often called the gateway to the Northeast. Assamese and Bodo are the official languages, and Bengali and Meitei are also official in some districts.

Assam is famous for its **tea**, its **silks** and its wildlife. It is home to more than half of the world's wild one-horned rhinoceroses, and three of its sites are on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

## History

In ancient times the region was known as **Kamarupa** or Pragjyotisha. The Chinese traveller Xuanzang visited it in the 7th century, during the reign of King Bhaskaravarman. After Kamarupa declined, several kingdoms shared the region, including the Chutia, Kachari and Koch kingdoms.

The **Ahom kingdom** was founded by the Tai prince Sukaphaa, who by traditional accounts entered the Brahmaputra valley in 1228 and established his capital at **Charaideo** in 1253. The Ahoms ruled much of the valley for about 600 years. They resisted Mughal expansion in the 17th century, most famously at the Battle of Saraighat in 1671.

In the early 19th century, the kingdom was weakened by civil war and Burmese invasions. After the First Anglo-Burmese War, the Treaty of Yandabo in 1826 brought Assam under the East India Company. The British began growing tea there in the 1830s. Assam later became a separate province, and after independence it became a state of the Indian Republic in 1950. In the following decades, several new states, including Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram, were formed from territory that had been part of Assam.

## Geography and climate

The heart of Assam is the **Brahmaputra valley**, a broad plain about 80 to 100 km wide that the river crosses from east to west. Many tributaries and oxbow lakes cross it. The **Barak valley** in the south is drained by the Barak River, which flows on into Bangladesh. Between them lie the hills of Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao. Invest India reports that about 37% of the state is recorded forest.

The climate is subtropical, with warm, humid summers and cool, dry winters. Invest India puts the average annual rainfall at about 2,300 mm, most of it falling in the monsoon from June to September. The Brahmaputra and Barak often flood in the monsoon.

## Economy

Assam produces more than half of India's tea, from the gardens of the Brahmaputra valley, according to Invest India. It is also known for its silks. Invest India says the state produces about 97% of India's **muga** silk, a golden silk unique to Assam, and about 65% of its **eri** silk.

Oil was discovered in Assam in 1867. Petroleum, natural gas, coal and limestone are still important, and Dibrugarh is a centre of the oil and gas industry. Agriculture, especially rice, and wildlife tourism are also major parts of the economy.

## Culture, art and festivals

Assam's culture brings together Assamese, Bodo, Mising, Karbi, Bengali and many other communities. The 15th- and 16th-century saint **Srimanta Sankardev** shaped Assamese Vaishnavism, whose satras (monasteries), particularly on Majuli, preserve music, drama and the classical **Sattriya** dance. The woven **gamosa** cloth is a symbol of Assamese identity and is given as a mark of respect.

The three **Bihu** festivals follow the farming year. Rongali or Bohag Bihu in April marks the new year, with Bihu dances and songs. Kati or Kongali Bihu in October is a quieter festival of lamps, and Magh or Bhogali Bihu in January is a harvest festival with feasts. The **Ambubachi Mela** at the Kamakhya Temple draws large numbers of pilgrims each June.

## Food

Assamese food uses rice, fish and fresh herbs, and is usually lightly spiced. **Masor tenga**, a sour fish curry, is a favourite. **Khar**, made with an alkaline liquid filtered from banana-peel ash, is a distinctive opening dish. Duck curries are popular, as are **pitha** (rice cakes), made especially for Bihu. A cup of strong **Assam tea** is part of daily life.

## Tourist spots

**Guwahati** is the main gateway, with the **Kamakhya Temple** in the city and **Hajo** across the river. **Kaziranga** lies to the east along the Brahmaputra, and **Manas** lies to the north-west, on the border with Bhutan. In Upper Assam, **Jorhat** is the base for the ferry to **Majuli**. **Sivasagar**, the old Ahom heartland, has the **Rang Ghar**, and the **Charaideo Moidams** are nearby.

## Education and universities

Major institutions include IIT Guwahati, AIIMS Guwahati, NIT Silchar and Tezpur University, a central university, along with Gauhati University and Dibrugarh University.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi International Airport in Guwahati is the main airport of the Northeast, with flights across India. Dibrugarh, Jorhat and Silchar also have airports.

**By rail:** Guwahati is the region's main railway hub, with trains from Delhi, Kolkata and other major cities.

**By road:** National highways link Guwahati with West Bengal and with the other northeastern states. Long bridges across the Brahmaputra, including the Bogibeel Bridge near Dibrugarh, connect its two banks.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Plan around the parks.** Kaziranga and Manas are usually open only from about November to April. Book safaris early in peak season.
- **Expect floods in the monsoon.** Heavy rain from June to September can cut roads and ferries, so allow extra time.
- **Check ferry times for Majuli.** Ferries from near Jorhat run to a timetable that can change with the river level.
- **See the tea country.** The tea gardens of Upper Assam, around Jorhat and Dibrugarh, are at their greenest after the spring rains.
