## Overview

Bihar lies in eastern India, on the fertile plain of the Ganges, which flows across it from west to east. It borders Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand and West Bengal, with Nepal to the north. It had about 104 million people at the 2011 Census in an area of 94,163 square kilometres, which made it the most densely populated state in India.

The capital, Patna, stands on the site of Pataliputra, the capital of the Maurya and Gupta empires. Bihar is one of the most important places in the world for Buddhists. The Buddha attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, and the monasteries of Nalanda and Vikramashila drew scholars from across Asia. The state also holds sacred sites of Jainism, Sikhism and Hinduism.

Hindi and Urdu are the official languages, and Bhojpuri, Maithili and Magahi are widely spoken.

## History

Ancient Bihar was the heart of the kingdom of **Magadha**, first ruled from Rajgriha (modern Rajgir) and later from Pataliputra (modern Patna). To the north lay Vajji, with its capital at Vaishali, often described as the first known republic in India. Both the Buddha and Mahavira, the founder figures of Buddhism and Jainism, lived and taught here in the 6th and 5th centuries BCE.

The **Maurya Empire**, founded in Magadha around 321 BCE by Chandragupta Maurya, was India's first great empire. Under his grandson Ashoka it covered most of the subcontinent and supported the spread of Buddhism. The **Gupta Empire**, which also arose in Magadha, presided over a period often called a golden age of Indian science, mathematics and art. The monastery-universities of Nalanda and Vikramashila flourished until they were destroyed around the end of the 12th century.

In the 16th century Sher Shah Suri, whose base was at Sasaram, founded the Sur Empire. Bihar later formed part of the Mughal Empire and then of the British Bengal Presidency. In 1912 Bihar and Orissa were separated from Bengal as a new province, an event now marked as Bihar Diwas on 22 March. The southern part of the state became the separate state of Jharkhand in 2000.

## Geography and climate

The Ganges divides the state into **North Bihar** and **South Bihar**. The northern rivers, including the Gandak, Bagmati and Kosi, flow down from the Himalayas in Nepal, and the Son joins from the south. These rivers make the plain very fertile, but they also flood parts of it regularly during the monsoon.

Almost all of Bihar is flat plain. In the far north-west, the forested foothills of the Someshwar and Dun ranges rise along the Nepal border. The state has 38 districts.

The climate is subtropical, with hot summers, a monsoon from June to September and cool winters.

## Economy

Agriculture is the mainstay of Bihar's economy. Rice, wheat and sugarcane are major crops. Bihar is India's fourth-largest producer of vegetables and a major grower of fruit, especially litchis and mangoes. According to Invest India, the state produces about 90% of the world's fox nuts (*makhana*).

Invest India also reports that Bihar recorded growth of 20.4% in 2021–22, the highest in the country that year, and lists food processing, leather, IT services and electronics as focus sectors.

## Culture, art and festivals

Bihar has several distinct cultural regions, including **Mithila** in the north, **Magadh** in the south and the **Bhojpuri**-speaking west. Mithila is known for **Madhubani painting**, a colourful folk-art style, and for the Maithili language and its literature. The state has a strong tradition of classical music, including the *dhrupad* styles of the Darbhanga and Bettiah gharanas.

**Chhath Puja**, dedicated to the Sun, is the most widely celebrated festival and is observed by Bihari communities around the world. The Buddhist, Jain and Sikh pilgrimage sites of Bodh Gaya, Pawapuri and Patna Sahib draw visitors throughout the year.

## Food

The best-known dish is **litti chokha**: roasted wheat balls stuffed with **sattu** (roasted gram flour) and served with mashed vegetables. Sattu is also made into drinks and parathas. Sweets include **khaja** and **thekua**, and **makhana** (fox nuts) is a speciality of Mithila. Rice, lentils and vegetables are everyday staples.

## Tourist spots

Most visitors follow the **Buddhist circuit**: **Bodh Gaya**, then **Rajgir** and **Nalanda**, which are close together, and on to **Vaishali** north of the Ganges. **Pawapuri**, sacred to Jains, is near Rajgir. In **Patna**, Takht Sri Patna Sahib is one of the holiest places in Sikhism. The **Tomb of Sher Shah Suri** is at Sasaram in the south-west, and **Valmiki National Park** lies in the far north-west.

## Education and universities

Bihar's history of learning is reflected in the modern **Nalanda University** at Rajgir, set up as an international university near the ancient site. Other institutions include IIT Patna, NIT Patna, Patna University and the Central University of South Bihar at Gaya.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Patna has the main airport. Gaya airport serves Bodh Gaya and has seasonal international flights for Buddhist pilgrims. Darbhanga has an airport in the north.

**By rail:** Patna, Gaya and Muzaffarpur are major stations on lines linking Delhi with Kolkata and the north-east.

**By road:** National highways connect Patna with the rest of the state and with neighbouring states. Bodh Gaya is about 96 km from Patna.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Alcohol is prohibited.** The sale and consumption of alcohol have been banned in Bihar since 2016.
- **Visit in winter.** October to March is the most comfortable time, and the main season at Bodh Gaya.
- **Avoid the monsoon in the north.** Flooding can disrupt travel between June and September.
- **Dress modestly at religious sites.** Cover your head in gurdwaras, and remove shoes at temples, monasteries and shrines.
