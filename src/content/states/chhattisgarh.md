## Overview

Chhattisgarh is a landlocked state in east-central India. It borders seven states: Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. It had about 25.5 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 135,192 square kilometres.

The capital is Raipur. The state was carved out of Madhya Pradesh on 1 November 2000. More than 40% of its land is forest, among the highest shares in India, and the southern region of **Bastar** is known for its Adivasi (tribal) culture, waterfalls and sal forests. The central plains along the Mahanadi are an important rice-growing area.

Hindi is the official language, and Chhattisgarhi is widely spoken.

## History

In ancient times the region was known as **Dakshina Kosala** (South Kosala). The Chinese traveller Xuanzang wrote that Ashoka built Buddhist stupas at Shripura, today's Sirpur, the region's ancient capital. Over the centuries Dakshina Kosala was ruled by dynasties including the Vakatakas, the Panduvanshis and the Kalachuris.

From the 11th century the **Kalachuris of Ratnapura**, later known as the Haihaiyavanshis, ruled most of the region for about 700 years. In 1741 it came under the Marathas, and the Bhonsles of Nagpur ruled it until 1845, when it was annexed by the East India Company. Under British rule it was the Chhattisgarh Division of the Central Provinces, with several princely states such as Bastar alongside it.

After independence, the region became part of Madhya Pradesh. The Madhya Pradesh Reorganisation Act of 2000 created the separate state of Chhattisgarh on **1 November 2000**.

## Geography and climate

The north of the state is a belt of hills formed by the eastern end of the Satpura Range and the edge of the Chota Nagpur Plateau. The highest point, Gaurlata, lies near Samri in Balrampur-Ramanujganj district. The fertile centre is the upper basin of the **Mahanadi** and its tributaries, such as the Shivnath, Hasdeo and Arpa. The south lies on the Deccan Plateau, drained by the **Indravati**, a tributary of the Godavari.

About 41% of the state is covered by forest, one of the highest proportions in India. The climate is hot and humid in summer, the monsoon brings most of the year's rain from June to September, and winters are mild.

## Economy

Chhattisgarh is rich in minerals and energy. It has the third-largest coal reserves in India and supplies electricity, coal and steel to the rest of the country. Invest India calls it the "power capital of India", with an installed generating capacity of more than 26,000 MW. Bhilai is home to one of India's largest steel plants.

Agriculture, especially rice on the central plains, employs much of the population, and forest products are important in the hill regions. Invest India lists IT services, agriculture, pharmaceuticals, textiles and electronics among the state's focus sectors.

## Culture, art and festivals

Chhattisgarh's culture blends the farming traditions of the plains with those of its many Adivasi communities, including the Gond, Halba and Muria. Folk forms include *Pandwani*, the sung retelling of the Mahabharata, and the *Raut Nacha* dance. The state is also known for *dhokra* metal casting and Bastar's crafts.

The most distinctive festival is **Bastar Dussehra**, a celebration lasting about 75 days in Jagdalpur centred on the goddess Danteshwari. Farming festivals such as **Hareli** and **Pola** mark the agricultural year, and the **Rajim Kumbh** fair is held each February at a river confluence near Raipur.

## Food

Rice is the staple, and many local dishes are made from rice flour. They include **chila** (savoury pancakes), **fara** (steamed dumplings) and sweets such as dehrori. Gram-flour dishes such as **bafauri** are popular. The forests of Bastar add their own ingredients, including bamboo shoots, wild greens and the tangy **chapda** chutney made from red ants.

## Tourist spots

**Raipur** is the main gateway, and **Sirpur** is about two hours to the east. In the south, **Jagdalpur** is the base for Bastar, including **Chitrakote Falls**, **Kanger Valley National Park** and **Tirathgarh Falls**, with **Dantewada** and its Danteshwari Temple further south. In the north-west, the **Bhoramdeo** temples stand near Kawardha. The cool plateau of **Mainpat** is in the far north.

## Education and universities

Major institutions include IIT Bhilai, NIT Raipur and IIM Raipur, along with Pt. Ravishankar Shukla University in Raipur and Guru Ghasidas Vishwavidyalaya, a central university in Bilaspur.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Swami Vivekananda Airport in Raipur is the main airport, with flights to major Indian cities. Jagdalpur has a smaller airport serving Bastar.

**By rail:** Raipur and Bilaspur lie on the main line between Mumbai and Kolkata. A separate line links Visakhapatnam with Jagdalpur.

**By road:** National highways link Raipur with Nagpur, Odisha and Jharkhand. Jagdalpur is about 300 km south of Raipur by road.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **See the waterfalls after the rains.** Chitrakote and Tirathgarh are most impressive from July to October.
- **Plan time in Bastar.** The waterfalls, temples and markets of the south need at least two or three days.
- **Check local advice for remote areas.** Some forested parts of the south are remote and have limited services, so ask your hotel or local authorities before travelling off the main routes.
- **Visit a haat (weekly market).** Bastar's village markets are a lively way to see local crafts and food.
