## Overview

Goa is the smallest state in India by area, at 3,702 square kilometres, and had about 1.46 million people at the 2011 Census. It sits on the west coast between Maharashtra to the north and Karnataka to the east and south, with the Arabian Sea to the west. Its capital is Panaji (also called Panjim), a small city on the south bank of the Mandovi River.

For most visitors Goa means beaches, but the state is more varied than that. Behind the 105 km coastline lie river estuaries, paddy fields, villages with whitewashed churches and temples, and the forested slopes of the Western Ghats. Four and a half centuries of Portuguese rule left a mark on its architecture, food and music that is unlike anywhere else in India.

Goa became part of India in 1961 and a full state in 1987. Konkani is the official language, and it is a popular destination for both domestic and international travellers.

## History

Goa's past goes back long before the Portuguese. The region formed part of the Maurya Empire in the 3rd century BC, and was later ruled by a series of dynasties, including the Kadambas, who governed from around 960 to 1310. It then passed through the hands of the Delhi Sultanate, the Vijayanagara Empire, the Bahmani Sultanate and the Adil Shahi rulers of Bijapur.

In 1510 a Portuguese fleet under Afonso de Albuquerque captured the city of Goa from the Bijapur Sultanate. Goa became the capital of Portugal's possessions in Asia, and the city now known as Old Goa grew into a major centre of trade and of the Catholic Church in the East. Portuguese rule lasted for about 450 years.

After India's independence in 1947, Portugal declined to hand over its territories. Indian forces took control on **19 December 1961**, now marked as Goa Liberation Day. Goa was then governed as part of the Union Territory of Goa, Daman and Diu. In a 1967 opinion poll, voters chose to keep Goa separate rather than merge it with Maharashtra. Konkani was made the official language in 1987, and Goa became a full state on **30 May 1987**.

## Geography and climate

Goa has three districts: North Goa, South Goa and Kushavati. Kushavati was created on 31 December 2025 from four talukas of South Goa: Dharbandora, Sanguem, Quepem and Canacona. The land rises from sea level on the coast to around 1,022 metres in the Western Ghats along the eastern border.

Six major rivers cross the state to the sea: the Mandovi, Zuari, Sal, Terekhol, Chapora and Talpona. The Mandovi and Zuari estuaries, linked by the Cumbarjua canal, form the heart of the state around Panaji.

The climate is warm and humid. Temperatures range from about 17 °C to 36 °C over the year. The south-west monsoon usually arrives in the first week of June and lasts until September, bringing average annual rainfall of about 300 cm. The countryside is at its greenest during this time.

## Economy

Tourism is Goa's biggest industry. Inland, iron ore, manganese and bauxite are mined, and much of the ore has historically been exported through Mormugao Port near Vasco da Gama. Agriculture (mainly rice, areca nut, cashew and coconut), fishing, and manufacturing, including pharmaceuticals, are also important.

Goa is one of India's most prosperous states. According to figures given in Parliament, it had the highest per-capita net state domestic product of any state or union territory in 2023–24, at ₹3,57,611 at current prices.

## Culture, art and festivals

Goan culture blends Konkani, Marathi and Portuguese influences. Hindus make up about 66% of the population, Christians about 25% and Muslims about 8% (Census 2011). Both Hindu and Christian festivals are celebrated widely.

Traditional music and dance forms include the *mando*, *fugdi*, *dekhnni* and *corridinho*. On stage, the most popular form is *tiatr*, Konkani musical theatre performed in the Roman script, which has helped keep the language and its music alive. Football is the most popular sport and has been played in Goa since the 1880s.

The state's festival calendar runs from Carnival and Shigmo in the spring to São João in the monsoon, Ganesh Chaturthi (known locally as *Chovoth*) in late summer, and the Feast of St Francis Xavier and Christmas in December.

## Food

Goan food is built on rice, seafood and coconut, and a meal is rarely considered complete without fish. Kokum and vinegar are distinctive ingredients. The cuisine has Konkani roots and was shaped by more than four centuries of Portuguese rule. Many Catholic Goan dishes, such as sorpotel and vindaloo, are variants of Portuguese originals.

Desserts include bebinca, a multi-layered coconut cake. Feni, a spirit distilled from cashew fruit or coconut sap, is the state's signature drink and has had Geographical Indication status since 2009.

## Tourist spots

The northern coast, around Calangute, Baga, Anjuna and Vagator, is the busiest stretch, with Fort Aguada and Chapora Fort on the headlands. South Goa's beaches, such as Palolem, tend to be quieter. Inland, Old Goa's churches are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed in 1986). The temples around Ponda and the forests and waterfalls of the Western Ghats are well worth the trip away from the sea.

## Education and universities

Goa University, the state university, is on the Taleigao plateau near Panaji. The state is also home to the National Institute of Technology Goa, the Indian Institute of Technology Goa, and the Goa campus of BITS Pilani.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Goa has two international airports. Goa International Airport at Dabolim, near Vasco da Gama, serves the south and centre of the state. Manohar International Airport at Mopa, in the far north, opened in December 2022 and began flight operations on 5 January 2023.

**By rail:** The Konkan Railway runs along the coast, connecting Goa with Mumbai to the north and Mangaluru and Kerala to the south. Main stations include Madgaon (Margao), Karmali (for Old Goa and Panaji) and Thivim (for North Goa). A separate South Western Railway line runs inland from Vasco da Gama through Margao towards Karnataka.

**By road:** National Highway 66 runs the length of the coast, linking Goa with Mumbai and Mangaluru. Interstate buses connect Goa with cities across Maharashtra and Karnataka.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Plan around the monsoon.** June to September brings heavy rain. It is the greenest and cheapest time to visit, but many beach activities slow down.
- **Pick the right airport.** Mopa is closest to the northern beaches. Dabolim is closer to Panaji, Margao and the south.
- **Dress respectfully at places of worship.** Cover shoulders and knees in churches and temples, and follow any posted rules on photography.
- **Allow time inland.** Old Goa, the Ponda temples and Dudhsagar Falls are each a short drive or train ride from the coast.
