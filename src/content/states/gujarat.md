## Overview

Gujarat is on India's west coast, bordered by the Arabian Sea, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and the union territory of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu. To the north-west it has an international border with Pakistan's Sindh province. The state had about 60 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 196,244 square kilometres. Its coastline, about 1,600 km by most official counts, is the longest of any Indian state.

The capital is Gandhinagar, and the largest city is Ahmedabad, whose old walled city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Gujarat is the home state of Mahatma Gandhi. It holds some of India's most important archaeological sites, from the Harappan cities of Dholavira and Lothal to the stepwells of Patan.

Gujarat was created on 1 May 1960, when Bombay State was divided into Gujarat and Maharashtra. Gujarati is the official language.

## History

Gujarat was home to major cities of the Indus Valley (Harappan) Civilisation, including Lothal, often described as one of the world's earliest ports, and Dholavira. The region later formed part of the Mauryan and Gupta empires and was ruled by the Western Satraps and the Maitrakas of Valabhi.

The Chaulukya (Solanki) dynasty ruled from 960 to 1243 and built many of the temples and stepwells that survive today, including Rani ki Vav. Gujarat then came under the Delhi Sultanate. In the early 15th century an independent Gujarat Sultanate emerged, and Sultan Ahmed Shah (who ruled from 1411 to 1442) made Ahmedabad its capital. The Mughal emperor Akbar annexed the region in 1572, and Surat became one of the main ports of Mughal India.

From the 18th century, the Marathas and later the British became the dominant powers. Much of the region was split among many princely states, including Baroda. After independence, Gujarat formed part of Bombay State until 1 May 1960, when the Bombay Reorganisation Act created the separate states of Gujarat and Maharashtra.

## Geography and climate

Gujarat has three distinct parts. The mainland stretches from the Rajasthan border to the Gulf of Khambhat. The **Saurashtra** (Kathiawar) peninsula is a broad plateau with the Gir forest and Mount Girnar. **Kutch** in the north-west is bordered by the salt marshes of the Great and Little Rann. Major rivers include the Narmada, Tapi, Mahi and Sabarmati.

The climate is mostly dry. Summers (April to June) are very hot, the monsoon arrives in late June, and winters are mild and sunny. The Rann of Kutch floods during the monsoon and dries into a white salt crust in winter.

## Economy

Gujarat is one of India's most industrialised states. According to Invest India, it accounted for 29.88% of India's exports in 2024–25 and produces 62% of the country's petrochemicals. It also ranks first in installed wind-power capacity. The coast has one major port and 48 non-major ports, which handle a large share of India's cargo.

Invest India also highlights its large industrial estates and special economic zones, including the petrochemical region at Dahej. Historically, Surat was a great trading port for silk and diamonds.

## Culture, art and festivals

Gujarati culture places great value on trade, food and community festivals. **Navratri** is the biggest festival of the year: nine nights of *garba* and *dandiya raas* dancing in towns and villages across the state. On 14 January, **Uttarayan** fills the skies with kites.

Jain communities have a long history in Gujarat, and the hills of Shatrunjaya (Palitana) and Girnar are among the most important Jain pilgrimage sites. Gujarat is one of a few Indian states that prohibit the sale of alcohol.

## Food

Gujarati food is mostly vegetarian, often lightly sweet and tangy. The **Gujarati thali** brings together dal, kadhi (a yoghurt curry), vegetables, rotis, rice, pickles and sweets on a single platter. Snacks (*farsan*) such as **dhokla**, **khandvi** and **fafda** are everyday staples. **Undhiyu** is a winter speciality.

## Tourist spots

Ahmedabad, with its old walled city and **Sabarmati Ashram**, is the usual starting point. Kutch has the **Rann of Kutch**, **Dholavira** and craft villages. Saurashtra has **Gir National Park**, the **Somnath** and Dwarka temples, and the hilltop Jain temples of Palitana. Closer to Vadodara are **Champaner-Pavagadh** and, on the Narmada, the **Statue of Unity**. **Rani ki Vav** and the Modhera Sun Temple are in the north.

## Education and universities

Ahmedabad is a major education centre. It is home to IIM Ahmedabad, CEPT University (known for architecture and planning) and Gujarat University, as well as the National Institute of Design. IIT Gandhinagar is nearby, and the Maharaja Sayajirao University in Vadodara is one of the state's oldest universities.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport in Ahmedabad is the main gateway, with domestic and international flights. Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot and Bhuj also have airports, and Bhuj is the nearest for Kutch.

**By rail:** Ahmedabad, Vadodara and Surat lie on the busy main line between Mumbai and Delhi. Rail links reach Saurashtra and Kutch.

**By road:** National highways connect Gujarat with Mumbai, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh. A good road network links the main cities, and intercity buses are frequent.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Plan for prohibition.** The sale of alcohol is prohibited in Gujarat, so check the current rules before you travel.
- **Book Gir safaris in advance.** Permits are limited, and the park closes during the monsoon.
- **Visit the Rann in winter.** The salt flats are dry and white from about November, and a permit may be needed for the border area.
- **Avoid the summer heat.** April to June is very hot in Kutch and the interior.
