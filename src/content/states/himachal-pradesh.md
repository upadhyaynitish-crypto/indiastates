## Overview

Himachal Pradesh is a mountain state in the western Himalayas, in north India. It borders the union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh to the north, Punjab to the west, Haryana to the south-west, Uttarakhand to the south-east and, briefly, Uttar Pradesh to the south. To the east it shares an international border with the Tibet Autonomous Region of China.

The state had about 6.9 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 55,673 square kilometres, much of it high and thinly populated. **Shimla** is the capital (the summer capital), and **Dharamshala** is the winter capital. Hindi is the official language and Sanskrit an additional official language, while most people speak one of the Western Pahari languages at home, such as Kangri, Mandeali or Kullui.

Himachal is known for its hill towns, apples, hydropower and trekking country, from green valleys such as Kullu and Kangra to the high, dry desert of **Spiti**.

## History

The hills were ruled for most of their history by small local kingdoms, among them Kangra, Chamba, Mandi, Bilaspur and Kullu, some of which accepted the overlordship of larger empires. The fort at Kangra changed hands several times: the Gurkhas of Nepal besieged it in the early 19th century, and it passed to Maharaja Ranjit Singh in 1809. Under British rule, much of the region was part of Punjab, while the British built **Shimla** into the summer capital of British India from 1864.

After independence, 30 small princely states were joined to form the Chief Commissioner's Province of Himachal Pradesh on 15 April 1948. It became a union territory in 1956, and in 1966 the hill areas of Punjab, including Kangra, Kullu and Shimla, were added to it. Himachal Pradesh became a full state, the 18th in India, on **25 January 1971**.

## Geography and climate

Almost all of Himachal is mountainous, running from the Shivalik foothills in the south through the Dhauladhar and Pir Panjal ranges to the Great Himalaya and Zanskar ranges in the north and east. Four of the five rivers of Punjab flow through the state: the **Sutlej**, **Beas**, **Ravi** and **Chenab**, along with the Yamuna on its south-eastern border.

The climate changes with height, from hot and subtropical in the lower hills to cold and alpine in the north. Dharamshala receives very heavy rainfall, while Lahaul and Spiti are cold and almost rainless. Most of the year's rain falls in the monsoon from June to September, and winter brings snow to the higher areas.

## Economy

Farming and horticulture employ much of the population. **Apples** are the main cash crop, grown mainly in Shimla, Kinnaur, Kullu and Chamba, and Invest India says they make up about three-quarters of the value of the state's horticulture. The Kangra Valley has grown tea since 1849.

Fast-flowing rivers give Himachal nearly a quarter of India's harnessable hydropower potential, according to Invest India, and the state sells surplus electricity to its neighbours. Invest India also says that about a third of all medicines made in India come from Himachal. Tourism is a major source of income.

## Culture, art and festivals

Festivals in Himachal's villages honour local gods and goddesses, and there are temple fairs in nearly every region. **Kullu Dussehra**, held on Kullu's Dhalpur ground, begins on the day Dussehra ends elsewhere in India. **Mandi Shivratri** and Chamba's **Minjar Fair** are other large gatherings. In Lahaul, Spiti and Kinnaur, Buddhist traditions and the **Losar** new year are central.

The state is known for its crafts, including Kullu shawls, Chamba *rumal* embroidery and the miniature paintings of the Kangra school.

## Food

Everyday food in Himachal is similar to the rest of north India, with Punjabi and Tibetan influences. Local specialities include **siddu** (stuffed steamed buns), **madra** (pulses in a yoghurt gravy), **chha gosht** (lamb in yoghurt), **mah ki dal** and Kullu trout. Many of these are served together at a *dham*, a traditional festive meal.

## Tourist spots

**Shimla** and **Manali** are the best-known hill towns. **McLeod Ganj**, above Dharamshala, is the centre of the Tibetan community and a base for treks into the Dhauladhar. In summer, roads open across the high passes to **Spiti**, with **Key Monastery**, and to the lake of **Chandratal**. The **Great Himalayan National Park** in Kullu district is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and **Kangra Fort** is one of the oldest forts in the Himalayas.

## Education and universities

Major institutions include IIT Mandi, NIT Hamirpur, IIM Sirmaur, AIIMS Bilaspur and the Central University of Himachal Pradesh in Dharamshala.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Small airports at Gaggal (for Dharamshala), Bhuntar (for Kullu and Manali) and Jubbarhatti (for Shimla) have flights mainly to Delhi and Chandigarh.

**By rail:** From Kalka, at the foot of the hills, the narrow-gauge **Kalka–Shimla Railway**, part of the UNESCO-listed Mountain Railways of India, climbs to Shimla. The Kangra Valley Railway runs from Pathankot to Jogindernagar.

**By road:** Buses and taxis connect Delhi and Chandigarh with Shimla, Manali and Dharamshala. Mountain roads are slow, so allow more time than distances suggest.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Check the passes before planning Spiti.** Roads to Lahaul and Spiti open only in summer and can close suddenly with snow or landslides.
- **Acclimatise at height.** Key Monastery is at about 4,166 m, and much of Spiti is higher still. Go up gradually and rest on your first day.
- **Avoid the monsoon for long drives.** July to September brings landslides that can block mountain roads for days.
- **Book ahead in peak season.** Popular hill towns such as Shimla and Manali fill up quickly in summer.
