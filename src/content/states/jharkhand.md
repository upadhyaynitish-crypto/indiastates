## Overview

Jharkhand, whose name means "land of forests", is a landlocked state in eastern India. It lies mostly on the Chota Nagpur Plateau and borders Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and West Bengal. It had about 33 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 79,716 square kilometres.

The capital is Ranchi. The state was created on 15 November 2000 from the southern part of Bihar. It is known for its waterfalls, forested hills and holy places, including the Baidyanath Temple at Deoghar and the Jain pilgrimage site of Parasnath. It has a large Adivasi (tribal) population, among them the Santal, Munda, Oraon and Ho peoples, whose languages, festivals and art shape its culture.

Hindi is the official language. Many other languages, including Santali, Mundari, Ho, Kurukh, Nagpuri, Khortha, Bengali, Odia and Urdu, have additional official status.

## History

Stone tools from the Chota Nagpur Plateau show that people have lived here since the Mesolithic period. In the Mauryan period the region was known for its forest states. For much of its later history it was ruled by local chiefs and small kingdoms. In the 18th century these came under the influence of the Marathas and then of the East India Company.

The 19th century saw a series of uprisings against colonial rule and the land system that came with it. They included the Santhal rebellion of 1855, led by the brothers Sidhu and Kanhu, and the movement led by **Birsa Munda** between 1895 and 1900. Birsa Munda is remembered today as one of the great Adivasi leaders.

The demand for a separate state for the plateau region was raised through the 20th century by leaders such as Jaipal Singh Munda. Jharkhand finally became a separate state on 15 November 2000, the anniversary of Birsa Munda's birth.

## Geography and climate

Most of Jharkhand lies on the **Chota Nagpur Plateau**, an upland of hills, valleys and forests. Rivers such as the Damodar, Subarnarekha, North Koel, South Koel and Barakar flow off the plateau, and where they drop over its edges they form waterfalls like Hundru and Dassam. Parasnath Hill, in Giridih district, is the highest mountain in the state. Nearly 30% of the state is covered by forest, which shelters elephants and tigers. There are 24 districts.

The south-west monsoon, from mid-June to October, brings nearly all of the year's rain, about 1,000 to 1,500 mm. Winters are cool and dry.

## Economy

Jharkhand is one of India's richest states in minerals. According to Invest India, it holds about 40% of the country's mineral reserves, including coal, iron ore, copper, mica, bauxite and uranium, and its mineral production was worth USD 8.7 billion in 2023–24. The state produces roughly 20 to 25% of India's steel.

Heavy industry grew up around these resources. **Jamshedpur** is the home of Tata Steel's main plant, and Bokaro and Dhanbad are major centres of steel and coal. Invest India lists metals and mining, capital goods, food processing and electric mobility among the state's focus sectors.

## Culture, art and festivals

Jharkhand's culture is deeply shaped by its Adivasi communities and their close relationship with the forest. Festivals follow the seasons. **Sarhul** celebrates the flowering of the sal trees in spring, **Karam** is held for a good harvest, and **Sohrai** marks the harvest and honours cattle. Durga Puja, Chhath, Holi and Diwali are also widely celebrated.

Folk dances include Jhumair, Domkach, Paika and **Chhau**, a masked dance tradition. **Sohrai and Khovar painting** is a mural art practised by women, traditionally at harvest time and at weddings. Films and music are produced in several regional languages, including Nagpuri, Khortha and Santali.

## Food

Everyday food is based on rice, lentils, vegetables and greens. Well-known dishes include **dhuska** (fried rice-and-lentil cakes), **chilka roti**, **pitha** and sweets such as malpua and arsa. **Rugra**, a wild mushroom gathered in the rainy season, is a local delicacy. **Handia**, a rice beer brewed in earthen pots, is the traditional drink of many Adivasi communities.

## Tourist spots

**Ranchi** is the base for the waterfalls of the plateau, including **Hundru** and **Dassam**, and for the drive through the **Patratu Valley**. To the west are the hill station of **Netarhat** and **Betla National Park**. In the north-east, **Deoghar** has the Baidyanath Temple, and **Parasnath Hill** is the site of the Jain shrines of Shikharji. **Jamshedpur**, in the south-east, is a planned industrial city with large parks and lakes.

## Education and universities

Jharkhand has several nationally known institutions, including IIT (ISM) Dhanbad, originally a school of mines, BIT Mesra near Ranchi, NIT Jamshedpur, IIM Ranchi and XLRI in Jamshedpur. Ranchi University is the main state university.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Birsa Munda Airport in Ranchi is the main airport, with flights to Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad. Deoghar also has an airport.

**By rail:** Ranchi, Jamshedpur (Tatanagar), Dhanbad and Bokaro are the main railway stations, with direct trains to Kolkata, Delhi and Patna.

**By road:** National highways link Ranchi with Patna, Kolkata, Jamshedpur and Dhanbad.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Time waterfall visits for after the rains.** The falls are most impressive from August to October, but rocks and paths can be slippery, so take care.
- **Carry warm clothes for Netarhat.** Nights on the plateau can be cold in winter.
- **Expect big crowds at Deoghar in Shravan.** July and August bring very large numbers of pilgrims.
- **Respect sacred sites.** At Shikharji, visitors are expected to follow Jain customs, which include not bringing leather or non-vegetarian food onto the hill.
