## Overview

Karnataka spans India's south-west, from a short Arabian Sea coast over the Western Ghats onto the dry Deccan Plateau. It had about 61 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 191,791 square kilometres. It is the only southern state that borders all four of the other southern states (Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana), as well as Goa and Maharashtra.

The capital, Bengaluru, is one of the world's largest technology hubs. Away from the city, Karnataka holds some of India's finest temple architecture: the ruins of Hampi, the Chalukya temples of Pattadakal and the intricately carved Hoysala temples of Belur and Halebidu.

The state was formed as Mysore State on 1 November 1956 and renamed Karnataka in 1973. Kannada, one of India's classical languages, is the official language.

## History

After the Mauryas and Satavahanas, the first local kingdoms in the region were the Kadambas, based at Banavasi, and the Western Gangas, based at Talakad. They were followed by the Badami Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas and the Western Chalukyas, empires that ruled much of the Deccan from capitals in present-day Karnataka. From around the 11th century the Hoysalas took control of the south, leaving behind the temples of Belur and Halebidu.

In the 14th century, Harihara and Bukka founded the Vijayanagara Empire, with its capital at Hampi on the Tungabhadra River. The empire fell after the Battle of Talikota in 1565. In 1537, Kempe Gowda I, a Vijayanagara chieftain, built a fort and town that grew into Bengaluru.

In the south, the Kingdom of Mysore became powerful under the Wadiyars and later under Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan, who fought four wars against the East India Company. After Tipu Sultan's death in 1799, Mysore became a princely state under the Wadiyars. After independence it joined India. In 1956 the Kannada-speaking areas of neighbouring states were added to form Mysore State, which became Karnataka in 1973.

## Geography and climate

Karnataka has three broad zones. The narrow **coast** (Karavali) runs along the Arabian Sea. The **Malnad**, the forested hills of the Western Ghats, is where the highest point, Mullayanagiri (1,925 m), stands in Chikkamagaluru district. The **Bayaluseeme** covers the plains of the Deccan Plateau.

The two great river systems are the Krishna, with its tributaries the Bhima, Ghataprabha, Malaprabha and Tungabhadra in the north, and the Kaveri (Cauvery), with the Hemavati and Kabini in the south. The coast and the Ghats receive very heavy rain in the south-west monsoon, from June to September.

## Economy

Bengaluru is the centre of India's technology industry. Invest India describes it as one of the world's largest tech clusters, home to over 500 global capability centres, and says Karnataka holds 54% of India's biotech workforce. The state attracted about 21% of India's foreign direct investment between October 2019 and September 2024.

Aerospace, electronics, pharmaceuticals and renewable energy are other growth sectors. Much of India's silk industry is based in the state. Agriculture remains important across the Deccan, and the Western Ghats districts are known for plantation crops. New Mangalore is the state's major port, alongside nine minor ports.

## Culture, art and festivals

Kannada literature goes back more than a thousand years. Between the 13th and 14th centuries the Haridasa saints composed devotional songs that shaped Carnatic music. Karnataka has contributed to both of India's classical music traditions, Carnatic in the south and Hindustani in the north of the state.

**Mysuru Dasara** is the *Nada Habba* (state festival), with the palace lit up and a grand procession through the city. Other major festivals include Ugadi (the Kannada New Year), Makara Sankranti, Ganesh Chaturthi, Gauri Habba and Deepavali.

## Food

Food varies a lot from region to region. The south is known for **ragi mudde** (millet balls) and rice dishes such as **bisi bele bath**. The north eats jowar rotis, and the coast is famous for seafood, coconut curries and the vegetarian cooking of **Udupi**, the home of many classic South Indian restaurant dishes. **Kodagu** has its own distinctive cuisine, and Mysuru gave its name to the sweet **Mysore pak**.

## Tourist spots

Bengaluru is the usual starting point, with Mysuru and its palace a few hours to the south-west. North Karnataka holds the ruins of **Hampi** and the temple towns of **Badami, Aihole and Pattadakal**. The **Hoysala temples** at Belur and Halebidu and the Jain pilgrimage site of **Shravanabelagola** are within reach of Hassan. The Western Ghats offer **Kodagu**, Chikkamagaluru and **Jog Falls**, and the south has tiger reserves at **Bandipur** and Nagarhole.

## Education and universities

Bengaluru has some of India's leading research and professional institutions, including the Indian Institute of Science, IIM Bangalore and the National Law School of India University. The University of Mysore is one of the oldest in the country, and NIT Karnataka at Surathkal is a leading engineering institute.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Kempegowda International Airport in Bengaluru is the state's main gateway and one of the busiest in India. Mangaluru has an international airport on the coast. Hubballi and Mysuru have domestic airports, and Hubballi is a practical base for Hampi and the north.

**By rail:** Bengaluru, Mysuru, Hubballi and Mangaluru are major railway hubs, with direct trains to Chennai, Hyderabad, Mumbai and Delhi. The Konkan Railway runs along the coast through Udupi and Karwar towards Goa and Mumbai.

**By road:** National highways link Bengaluru with Chennai, Hyderabad, Mumbai and Kerala. Good roads connect Mysuru, Hassan and Kodagu.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Give Hampi at least two days.** The site is spread over many square kilometres. Bicycles and auto-rickshaws are the usual way to get around.
- **Plan Mysuru around Dasara.** It's the most spectacular time to visit, and the busiest, so book early.
- **Dress respectfully at temples.** Cover shoulders and knees, remove shoes before entering, and follow posted rules on photography.
- **Allow extra time in Bengaluru traffic,** especially to and from the airport.
