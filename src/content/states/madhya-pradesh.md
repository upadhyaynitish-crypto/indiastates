## Overview

Madhya Pradesh, whose name means "central province", lies at the heart of India. It is the country's second-largest state by area, covering 308,252 square kilometres, and had about 72.6 million people at the 2011 Census. It is landlocked, bordered by Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra and Gujarat.

The capital is Bhopal, and other major cities include Indore, Gwalior and Jabalpur. The state is known for three UNESCO World Heritage Sites (Khajuraho, Sanchi and Bhimbetka), for forts and palaces such as Gwalior, Orchha and Mandu, and for some of India's best-known tiger reserves.

Madhya Pradesh took its present form on 1 November 1956. In 2000 its south-eastern region became the separate state of Chhattisgarh. Hindi is the official language.

## History

People have lived here since the Stone Age, as the paintings in the Bhimbetka rock shelters show. By the 6th century BCE, Ujjain had grown into a major city as the capital of the Avanti kingdom. The region later formed part of the Mauryan Empire, under which the Great Stupa at Sanchi was begun, and of the Gupta Empire.

In the medieval period, the Paramaras ruled Malwa and the Chandelas ruled Bundelkhand, where they built the temples of Khajuraho. Gond kingdoms held the forested centre and east. After the Delhi Sultanate weakened, regional states re-emerged, including the Tomars of Gwalior and the Sultanate of Malwa, with its capital at Mandu. The region later came under the Mughals. In the 18th century the Marathas took control of most of it, with the Holkars ruling from Indore and the Scindias from Gwalior.

After the Third Anglo-Maratha War, the British grouped the region into the Central Provinces and a number of princely states. After independence these became several states, which were merged on 1 November 1956 to form Madhya Pradesh, with Bhopal as its capital. Chhattisgarh was separated in November 2000.

## Geography and climate

The state straddles the Narmada River, which flows west between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges. These hills and the river are the traditional boundary between north and south India. The highest point is Dhupgarh (1,350 m), near the hill station of Pachmarhi in the Satpuras. Other important rivers include the Chambal, Betwa, Son and Tapti.

Broad regions include **Malwa** in the west, **Bundelkhand** and **Baghelkhand** in the north and north-east, and **Mahakoshal** and the Satpura hills in the south. The year has three main seasons: a hot summer, the monsoon, and a cool, dry winter.

## Economy

Agriculture is the mainstay. Madhya Pradesh leads India in the production of pulses, oilseeds and soybean, and wheat, gram, rice, maize and cotton are other major crops. The state is rich in minerals, with India's largest reserves of diamonds and copper.

Indore, Bhopal, Gwalior and Jabalpur are the main cities. Invest India notes the state's growing information-technology exports, its large solar parks and its eight commercial airports. Forests cover a large share of the land.

## Culture, art and festivals

Madhya Pradesh has a large tribal population, including the Gond, Bhil, Baiga and Korku communities, whose art, dance and festivals are central to the state's identity. Folk dances include Rai, Karma, Saila and Bhagoria.

The state is also one of the homes of Hindustani classical music. The Gwalior, Maihar and Senia gharanas are among its noted schools, and the 16th-century musician Tansen was born near Gwalior, where an annual music festival is held in his name. The **Khajuraho Dance Festival** brings classical dancers to the temples each winter, and Ujjain hosts the **Simhastha Kumbh Mela** every twelve years.

## Food

Food varies by region. **Malwa**, around Indore and Ujjain, is known for snacks and street food, including poha with jalebi, **bhutte ka kees** and sabudana khichdi. **Dal bafla**, wheat dumplings with lentils, is the hearty meal of the west.

## Tourist spots

**Bhopal** is the base for **Sanchi** and **Bhimbetka**. In the north, **Gwalior** and **Orchha** lead on to **Khajuraho**. In the west, **Indore** is the gateway to **Mandu**, **Ujjain** and the river temples of Omkareshwar and Maheshwar. The forests of the east and south hold the tiger reserves of **Kanha**, **Bandhavgarh**, Pench, Panna and Satpura, and the hill station of Pachmarhi.

## Education and universities

National institutes in the state include IIT Indore, IIM Indore, IISER Bhopal and the Maulana Azad National Institute of Technology in Bhopal. Dr. Harisingh Gour Vishwavidyalaya in Sagar is a central university.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Indore and Bhopal are the main airports. Jabalpur, Gwalior and Khajuraho also have flights, which are useful for the tiger reserves and the temples.

**By rail:** Because of its central position, many of India's main north–south and east–west railway lines cross the state. Bhopal, Itarsi, Jabalpur, Gwalior and Indore are important stations.

**By road:** National highways link the state with Delhi, Mumbai, Nagpur and Ahmedabad. Distances between sights are long, so allow plenty of travel time.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Book safaris early.** Entry to the tiger reserves is by limited permits, and core zones close during the monsoon.
- **Plan for long distances.** The main sights are spread across a very large state, so group them by region.
- **Start early in summer.** April to June is very hot, though it is the best time for seeing tigers.
- **Check closing days.** Some monuments and museums close one day a week, so confirm before you travel.
