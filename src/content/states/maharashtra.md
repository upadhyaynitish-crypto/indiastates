## Overview

Maharashtra is India's second most populous state, with about 112 million people at the 2011 Census, and its third largest by area. It stretches from a 720 km Arabian Sea coastline across the Western Ghats onto the Deccan Plateau. Its neighbours are Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Telangana, Karnataka and Goa, plus the union territory of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu.

The capital, Mumbai, is India's financial and entertainment centre, and Nagpur serves as the winter capital. Beyond the cities lies one of the country's richest concentrations of heritage. The Ajanta, Ellora and Elephanta caves, Mumbai's Victorian and Art Deco buildings, and a chain of Maratha forts are all UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Marathi is the official language, and the state was formed on 1 May 1960, a date still celebrated as Maharashtra Day.

## History

The region was ruled by the Mauryas in the 4th and 3rd centuries BCE, and from around 230 BCE by the Satavahanas for about four centuries. Later dynasties included the Vakatakas, the Chalukyas (6th to 8th centuries CE) and the Rashtrakutas (8th to 10th centuries), whose reign saw the carving of the Kailasa temple at Ellora. The Seuna Yadavas ruled much of the region until the early 14th century, when it came under the Delhi Sultanate, and later the Bahmani and Deccan sultanates.

In the 17th century, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj founded the Maratha kingdom and was crowned at Raigad in 1674. Under the Peshwas, based in Pune, Maratha power spread across much of India in the 18th century. It ended with the Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1818), after which much of the region was governed by the British as part of the Bombay Presidency.

After independence, Bombay State brought together Marathi- and Gujarati-speaking areas. The Samyukta Maharashtra movement campaigned for a separate Marathi-speaking state with Mumbai as its capital. On **1 May 1960**, the Bombay Reorganisation Act divided Bombay State into Maharashtra and Gujarat. Maharashtra has a long-standing border dispute with Karnataka over Belagavi and surrounding areas, and a petition on the matter is pending before the Supreme Court.

## Geography and climate

Maharashtra covers 307,713 square kilometres and is divided into six revenue divisions (Konkan, Pune, Nashik, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Amravati and Nagpur) and 36 districts. People also speak of five broad regions: the coastal **Konkan**, **Western Maharashtra**, **Khandesh** in the north, **Marathwada** and **Vidarbha** in the east.

The Western Ghats, known here as the Sahyadri, run parallel to the coast and separate the narrow Konkan strip from the Deccan Plateau. The highest point is Kalsubai, at 1,646 metres. The Godavari and the Krishna are the state's two major rivers, both rising in the Sahyadri and flowing east.

The year has three seasons: summer from March to June, the monsoon from June to September, and winter from November to January. Rainfall varies enormously. The Konkan coast receives more than 3,000 mm a year, while areas just 150 km inland, in the rain shadow of the Ghats, get only 500–700 mm.

## Economy

Maharashtra is India's largest state economy. In 2023–24 it produced 13.5% of India's nominal GDP, the highest share of any state. Per-capita income was estimated at ₹3,09,340 for 2024–25 in the state's Economic Survey.

Mumbai is the country's financial capital and home to the Reserve Bank of India, the Bombay Stock Exchange, the National Stock Exchange and SEBI. It is also the largest centre of India's film and television industry. Invest India lists automobiles and auto components, electric mobility, renewable energy, electronics and IT services among the state's focus sectors. Industry is concentrated around Mumbai, Thane, Pune, Nashik, Nagpur and Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar.

Agriculture remains important across the Deccan. Maharashtra had more than half of India's jowar (sorghum) area and about a third of its cotton area in 2014–17, and sugarcane is a major crop. The coast has two major ports and about 53 minor ones.

## Culture, art and festivals

Marathi literature dates back at least to the 13th century and the *Dnyaneshwari*, a commentary on the Bhagavad Gita. The Warkari devotional tradition, with its *abhangas* (devotional songs) and the annual walking pilgrimage to Pandharpur, remains one of the state's most visible cultural practices.

*Lavani* is a popular folk dance and *Tamasha* a traditional form of folk theatre, and modern Marathi theatre dates back to the mid-19th century. Cricket is the most popular spectator sport, while kabaddi, kho-kho and wrestling (*kusti*) have deep rural roots.

Festivals mix the religious and the civic: Gudi Padwa for the new year, the ten days of Ganeshotsav, Diwali, and Shiv Jayanti, which honours Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj.

## Food

Everyday food across the Deccan is built on wheat, rice, jowar and bajra flatbreads, lentils and vegetables. Regional cooking varies a lot. The Konkan coast eats rice and seafood, often in **Malvani** coconut curries. **Kolhapur** is known for fiery meat dishes, and **Vidarbha** has its own *Varhadi* cooking.

Mumbai's street food is famous in its own right. Vada pav, pav bhaji and misal pav are eaten all over the state.

## Tourist spots

Mumbai is the usual starting point, with Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, the Gateway of India and the Elephanta Caves a ferry ride away. Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (formerly Aurangabad) is the base for Ajanta and Ellora. The Sahyadri hold the Maratha forts and hill stations such as Mahabaleshwar, Lonavala and Matheran, and the Konkan coast has beaches and sea forts. In the east, Vidarbha's forests include the Tadoba Andhari and Melghat tiger reserves.

## Education and universities

Maharashtra has a large higher-education system, including the University of Mumbai, Savitribai Phule Pune University and the Nagpur and Marathwada universities. National institutes include IIT Bombay, IISER Pune and VNIT Nagpur.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport in Mumbai is the state's busiest. Navi Mumbai International Airport, inaugurated on 8 October 2025, is the region's second airport. Pune and Nagpur also have international airports, and the airport at Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar serves Ajanta and Ellora.

**By rail:** India's first passenger train ran from Mumbai to Thane in April 1853. Today the Central and Western Railway zones are headquartered in Mumbai. The Konkan Railway runs south along the coast towards Goa and Kerala, and long-distance trains connect Mumbai, Pune and Nagpur with most of the country.

**By road:** Maharashtra has one of India's largest road networks. National highways link it with Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka and Goa.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Plan around the monsoon.** June to September brings very heavy rain to Mumbai and the Konkan. It's a beautiful time in the Ghats, but expect delays and closed trails.
- **Book Ajanta and Ellora as a pair.** Both are reached from Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar. Allow a full day for each, and check weekly closing days before you go.
- **Use Mumbai's suburban trains outside rush hour.** They are fast and cheap, but extremely crowded at peak times.
- **Check safari dates.** Tiger reserves such as Tadoba run seasonal safaris that need advance booking, and some zones close in the monsoon.
