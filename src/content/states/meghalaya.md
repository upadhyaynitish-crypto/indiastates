## Overview

Meghalaya is a small hill state in north-east India. It borders Bangladesh to the south and west and the Indian state of Assam to the north and east. It had about 3 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 22,429 square kilometres. The capital is **Shillong**.

The name means "the abode of clouds" in Sanskrit, and with good reason: the southern Khasi Hills are among the wettest places on Earth. Most people belong to the Khasi, Garo and Jaintia (Pnar) peoples, who have historically followed a **matrilineal** system, in which family name and inheritance pass through women. English is the official language, alongside Khasi and Garo.

## History

People have lived in the Meghalaya hills since the Neolithic period. The Khasi, Garo and Jaintia peoples had their own kingdoms and chiefdoms until the British took control in the 19th century. The eastern hills were part of the medieval Jaintia Kingdom.

Under British rule the area was part of Assam, and it stayed part of Assam after independence. A movement for a separate hill state began in 1960. Meghalaya became an autonomous state within Assam on 2 April 1970, and a full state on **21 January 1972**. Its name was coined in 1936 by the geographer S. P. Chatterjee.

## Geography and climate

Meghalaya is a plateau of ancient rock, rising to 1,961 m at **Shillong Peak**. The Garo Hills in the west are lower, while the Khasi and Jaintia Hills in the centre and east are higher. In the south, rivers have cut deep gorges and many waterfalls, and the limestone hills hide caves such as Siju. Invest India reports that about three-quarters of the state is forest, with 352 kinds of orchid. Some ancient **sacred groves** have been protected by communities for centuries.

The south-west monsoon brings extreme rain. Invest India puts the average around Cherrapunji and Mawsynram at about 12,670 mm a year. Sohra (Cherrapunji) holds the records for the most rain in a calendar month and in a year, while nearby Mawsynram now has the highest average. Shillong is cool all year, and winters in the Khasi Hills can drop to about 2°C.

## Economy

Meghalaya's economy is mainly agricultural, growing potatoes, rice, maize, pineapples, bananas and spices, including the prized **Lakadong turmeric**. The hills are rich in minerals: Invest India calls Meghalaya the largest producer of coal and limestone in the North-East, and there are uranium and sillimanite deposits. The state is also a trade link with Bangladesh, and tourism is growing.

## Culture, art and festivals

Meghalaya is one of three Indian states with a Christian majority. Indigenous faiths, including Niam Khasi, Niamtre and Songsarek, are followed by about 9% of people, and Hindus are the largest religious minority. Festivals follow the farming year: the Khasi **Shad Suk Mynsiem** in spring and **Nongkrem** in autumn, the Pnar **Behdienkhlam** in July, and the Garo **Wangala**, the harvest festival of the "Hundred Drums", later in the year.

## Food

Well-known Khasi dishes include **jadoh** (rice cooked with meat), **dohneiiong** (pork in black sesame) and **tungrymbai** (fermented soybeans).

## Tourist spots

**Shillong** is the base for most trips, with **Elephant Falls** on its edge. South of the capital, **Sohra (Cherrapunji)** sits above gorges with waterfalls such as **Nohkalikai**, and a long walk down leads to the **living root bridges** of Nongriat. Further east, **Mawlynnong** village and the clear **Umngot River** at **Dawki**, on the Bangladesh border, are popular day trips.

## Education and universities

Major institutions include North-Eastern Hill University, a central university in Shillong, IIM Shillong and NIT Meghalaya in Sohra.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Shillong Airport at Umroi, about 30 km from the city, is a domestic airport. Guwahati's larger airport in Assam is the other option, followed by a road journey.

**By rail:** Meghalaya has very little railway. There is a station at Mendipathar in North Garo Hills, but Shillong and the Khasi Hills are reached by road, usually from Guwahati.

**By road:** Meghalaya has about 1,170 km of national highways. Roads link Guwahati with Shillong and run on to Sohra, Dawki and the other hill towns.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Carry rain gear whatever the season.** Even outside the monsoon, the southern hills can be wet and misty.
- **Be ready for a steep walk.** The path down to Nongriat's root bridges and back is long and steep, so allow plenty of time.
- **Respect village rules.** Follow any local signs, keep to the paths and take your rubbish with you.
- **Plan Dawki for the dry season.** The Umngot River is usually at its clearest outside the monsoon.
