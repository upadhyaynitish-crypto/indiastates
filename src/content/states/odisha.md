## Overview

Odisha lies on India's east coast, along the Bay of Bengal. It borders West Bengal and Jharkhand to the north, Chhattisgarh to the west and Andhra Pradesh to the south. It had about 42 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 155,707 square kilometres. The state has one of India's largest tribal populations.

The capital is Bhubaneswar, known for its many medieval temples. Odisha is famous for the Sun Temple at Konark, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and for the Jagannath Temple at Puri, the focus of the great Rath Yatra chariot festival. The state also has long beaches, the vast Chilika lagoon and forests rich in wildlife.

Odisha was created as the province of Orissa on 1 April 1936, uniting the Odia-speaking areas on the basis of language. Its English name changed from Orissa to Odisha in 2011. Odia is the official language.

## History

Ancient Odisha was the kingdom of **Kalinga**. In 261 BCE the Mauryan emperor Ashoka invaded it in the Kalinga War, whose bloodshed is said to have deeply affected him. Around 150 BCE the Jain king **Kharavela** made Kalinga a major power once more.

Later the **Eastern Ganga dynasty** ruled the region and built the great temples of Puri and Konark. Their capital was moved to Cuttack around 1135, and Cuttack remained the capital until 1948. The Eastern Gangas were followed by the **Gajapati** kings. The region later came under the Mughals and, in the 18th century, the Marathas. The British took the coastal Puri–Cuttack region from the Marathas in 1803.

In 1912 Bihar and Orissa were separated from Bengal as one province, and on **1 April 1936** Orissa became a province of its own, uniting Odia-speaking districts from several provinces. After independence the princely states of the region joined the state. Bhubaneswar became the capital in 1948.

## Geography and climate

Odisha has a fertile coastal plain, crossed by the deltas of rivers such as the **Mahanadi**, Brahmani, Baitarani and Subarnarekha, and a hilly, forested interior that includes part of the **Eastern Ghats**. The highest point is Deomali (1,672 m) in Koraput district. Chilika, near the coast, is one of the largest coastal lagoons in the world. The state has 30 districts.

The year has four seasons: winter (January to February), a hot pre-monsoon season (March to May), the south-west monsoon (June to September) and the north-east monsoon (October to December). The coast is exposed to cyclones from the Bay of Bengal.

## Economy

Odisha is rich in minerals and is one of India's main industrial states for metals. According to Invest India, it ranks first in India for the production of aluminium, steel and stainless steel. Paradip, its main port, handled 17.62% of India's bulk cargo in 2024–25.

Per-capita income was about ₹1.87 lakh in 2025–26. Agriculture, especially rice, and fishing remain important across the coastal plain. Invest India lists tourism, textiles and food processing among the state's focus sectors.

## Culture, art and festivals

Odia, one of India's classical languages, has a long literary tradition. The state is the home of **Odissi**, one of India's classical dance forms, and of fine crafts such as *Pattachitra* scroll painting, silver filigree from Cuttack and handloom textiles.

Life in much of the state revolves around the worship of Jagannath at Puri. The **Rath Yatra** each June or July, when the deities are drawn through the streets on giant chariots, is one of India's best-known festivals. Other major celebrations include Raja Parba, Nuakhai in western Odisha, Durga Puja and the Bali Jatra fair in Cuttack.

## Food

Odia food is built on rice, lentils, vegetables and fish, cooked with mustard and light spices. **Dalma**, lentils cooked with vegetables, is the classic home dish. **Pakhala bhata**, rice soaked in water, is a summer favourite. Odisha is known for its sweets made from fresh cheese (*chhena*), including **chhena poda** and **rasagola**. At Puri, the temple kitchens prepare **Mahaprasad**, offered to Jagannath and then shared with devotees.

## Tourist spots

The "golden triangle" of **Bhubaneswar**, **Puri** and **Konark** is the heart of most trips. It takes in the temples of Bhubaneswar, the Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves and Dhauli, the Jagannath Temple and beach at Puri, and the Sun Temple at Konark. **Chilika Lake** is a short drive south of Puri. For wildlife, **Bhitarkanika** has mangroves, and **Simlipal** in the north is a forested tiger reserve.

## Education and universities

Bhubaneswar is the state's main centre of higher education, with IIT Bhubaneswar, NISER and Utkal University. NIT Rourkela and IIM Sambalpur are in the west of the state.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Biju Patnaik International Airport in Bhubaneswar is the main airport, and the most convenient for Puri and Konark. Jharsuguda serves western Odisha.

**By rail:** Bhubaneswar, Cuttack and Puri are well connected by rail to Kolkata, Chennai, Delhi and other major cities.

**By road:** National highways run along the coast, linking Odisha with Kolkata and Andhra Pradesh. Good roads connect Bhubaneswar, Puri and Konark.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Check temple entry rules.** Entry to the Jagannath Temple at Puri is restricted to Hindus, and non-Hindu visitors can view the temple from outside.
- **Plan around the Rath Yatra.** It is a spectacular time to be in Puri, but book accommodation well ahead.
- **Watch for cyclone warnings.** October to December can bring storms on the coast.
- **Visit Chilika in winter.** Migratory birds are at their peak from about November to February.
