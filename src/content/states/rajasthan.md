## Overview

Rajasthan is India's largest state by area, covering 342,239 square kilometres in the north-west of the country. It had about 69 million people at the 2011 Census. Its neighbours are Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat, and it shares a long international border with Pakistan to the west.

Much of the west lies in the Thar Desert, while the Aravalli hills cross the state from south-west to north-east. Rajasthan is famous for the forts and palaces of its former princely states. Jaipur, the capital, is known as the Pink City, and its walled city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, as are six hill forts and the Keoladeo bird sanctuary.

The state was formed on 30 March 1949, now celebrated as Rajasthan Day, and reached its present shape in 1956. Hindi is the official language, and many people speak Rajasthani languages such as Marwari and Mewari at home.

## History

The region has some of India's earliest settlements, including the Indus Valley sites of Kalibangan and Balathal. From the early medieval period it was ruled by Rajput clans, whose kingdoms included Mewar (Chittorgarh and later Udaipur), Marwar (Jodhpur), Dhundhar (Jaipur), Bikaner and Jaisalmer.

Over the following centuries, the Rajput states fought, allied with and sometimes resisted the Delhi Sultanate and then the Mughal Empire. Mewar's rulers, including Rana Sanga and Maharana Pratap, are remembered for their long resistance. Other kingdoms, such as Amber (Jaipur), became close allies of the Mughals. In the 18th century the Marathas extended their influence into the region, and in the early 19th century most Rajput states entered treaties with the British, becoming princely states of the Rajputana Agency.

After independence, the princely states joined India. They were merged in stages into the State of Rajasthan, formed on 30 March 1949, and the merger was completed with the addition of Ajmer and other areas on 1 November 1956.

## Geography and climate

The Aravalli Range runs for more than 850 km across the state, from Guru Shikhar near Mount Abu, its highest point at 1,722 m, to Khetri in the north-east. About three-fifths of the state lies north-west of the hills, mostly in the **Thar Desert**. The east and south-east are more fertile and are drained by the Chambal and Banas rivers. The Luni River flows south-west towards the Rann of Kutch, and the Indira Gandhi Canal irrigates the north-west.

The climate is dry. Summers (April to June) are extremely hot, especially in the desert. Most rain falls in the monsoon (July to September), mainly in the east and south, and winters are pleasant by day and cold at night.

## Economy

According to Invest India, Rajasthan's economy was estimated at ₹18.75 lakh crore in 2025–26. Tourism, mining, renewable energy, automobiles and textiles are among its focus sectors. The state ranks first in India for marble production and processing. It also has rich deposits of zinc, copper and salt, including the Sambhar salt lake.

Rajasthan is a leader in solar power. Its sunny desert is home to the Bhadla Solar Park, one of the world's largest clusters of solar plants. Agriculture depends heavily on the monsoon and on irrigation from the Indira Gandhi Canal.

## Culture, art and festivals

Rajasthan's courts patronised architecture, music and miniature painting (Bundi is known for its miniature paintings). The **Ghoomar** and **Kalbelia** dances have gained international recognition.

The festival calendar includes **Gangaur** and **Teej**, celebrated especially by women, the **Pushkar Fair**, the **Desert Festival** in Jaisalmer and the **Urs** at Ajmer.

## Food

Desert cooking was designed for long keeping and scarce water. It relies on millets, lentils, gram flour, dried beans and berries, and dairy. **Dal baati churma** is the signature dish, and **gatte ki sabzi** and **ker sangri** are everyday favourites. Royal kitchens produced rich meat dishes such as **laal maas**. Jodhpur is famous for snacks like **mirchi bada** and **pyaaz kachori**, and for the sweet **ghevar**.

## Tourist spots

Most first-time visitors start in **Jaipur**, with Amer Fort, the Hawa Mahal and the Jantar Mantar, and continue to **Jodhpur** (Mehrangarh), **Jaisalmer** (the fort and the dunes of Sam), **Udaipur** (City Palace and the lakes) and **Chittorgarh**. Nature lovers head to **Ranthambore** for tigers and **Keoladeo** for birds. Pilgrims visit **Pushkar**, **Ajmer Sharif** and the **Dilwara Temples** at Mount Abu.

## Education and universities

Rajasthan's leading institutions include IIT Jodhpur, BITS Pilani, one of India's best-known private engineering institutes, MNIT Jaipur and IIM Udaipur. The University of Rajasthan is the main public university in Jaipur.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Jaipur International Airport is the main gateway, with domestic and some international flights. Udaipur, Jodhpur and Jaisalmer also have airports, though some Jaisalmer services are seasonal.

**By rail:** Jaipur, Ajmer, Jodhpur, Udaipur and Kota are well connected by rail to Delhi, Mumbai and Ahmedabad.

**By road:** National highways link Jaipur with Delhi, Agra and Ahmedabad, and good roads connect the main tourist cities. Many visitors hire a car and driver for a multi-city trip.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Avoid the peak of summer.** April to June is extremely hot, so plan sightseeing for early mornings and evenings if you travel then.
- **Pack layers in winter.** Desert nights in December and January can be close to freezing.
- **Buy composite tickets** where offered, for example for Jaipur's monuments. It saves time and money.
- **Dress modestly at religious sites.** Cover your head at the Ajmer Dargah and gurudwaras, and remove leather items at Jain temples where asked.
