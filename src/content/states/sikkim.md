## Overview

Sikkim is a small state in the Eastern Himalaya in north-east India. It borders Nepal to the west, the Tibet Autonomous Region of China to the north and north-east, and Bhutan to the east. West Bengal is its only neighbouring Indian state, to the south. Sikkim is India's least populous state, with about 610,000 people at the 2011 Census, and the second-smallest by area at 7,096 square kilometres.

The capital and largest city is Gangtok. Sikkim is home to **Kangchenjunga**, India's highest peak and the third-highest mountain in the world. Its landscapes range from subtropical valleys to alpine meadows and glaciers. Invest India describes it as India's first fully organic state, and it ranked first among states for per capita net state domestic product in 2023–24.

Nepali is the most widely spoken language. The state recognises eleven other official languages, including Sikkimese (Bhutia), Lepcha and Limbu, and English is widely used in government.

## History

The **Lepchas** are considered the earliest inhabitants of Sikkim, with the Limbus and Magars also living in the west and south from early times. The Buddhist teacher Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche) is said to have passed through the region in the 8th century.

In **1642**, Phuntsog Namgyal was consecrated as the first **Chogyal**, or priest-king, founding the Namgyal dynasty that ruled Sikkim for more than 300 years. The early capitals were at Yuksom and then Rabdentse, near today's Pelling. Through the 18th and 19th centuries the kingdom fought wars with its neighbours and lost territory, including the Darjeeling area, which was annexed to British India. Sikkim became a British protectorate, formalised by a convention in 1890.

After Indian independence, a treaty in 1950 made Sikkim a protectorate of India. In 1975, following a referendum, the monarchy was abolished and Sikkim became the **22nd state of India on 16 May 1975**.

## Geography and climate

Almost all of Sikkim is mountainous. Elevations range from about 280 metres on the border with West Bengal to 8,586 metres at the summit of Kangchenjunga on the border with Nepal. Snow-fed streams join to form the **Teesta** River and its tributary, the **Rangeet**, which flow from north to south through the state. Sikkim has many glacial lakes and hot springs.

The climate changes with altitude: subtropical in the southern valleys, temperate in most inhabited areas, and alpine to tundra in the far north. According to Invest India, most inhabited areas seldom go above 28°C in summer. The monsoon is heavy, and Sikkim is one of the few Indian states to receive regular snowfall.

## Economy

Sikkim's economy rests on agriculture, tourism and hydropower. Farmers grow rice on terraced fields, along with maize, millet, oranges, tea and **large cardamom**, of which Sikkim is India's largest producer. The state began moving to organic farming in 2003 and is recognised as India's first fully organic state.

Hydroelectric power from Sikkim's rivers is a major source of income: Invest India puts the state's large hydropower capacity at about 2,280 MW. Tourism has also grown quickly in recent decades.

## Culture, art and festivals

Sikkim's people include Nepali-speaking communities, who form the majority, as well as the Bhutia and Lepcha peoples. Hinduism is the largest religion, and Vajrayana **Buddhism** shapes much of public life, with monasteries, prayer flags and chortens across the state. The traditional Lepcha religion, Mun, is still practised.

The Buddhist calendar includes **Losar** (Tibetan New Year), **Saga Dawa** and **Bhumchu** at Tashiding. Hindu festivals such as **Dashain** and **Tihar** are celebrated widely, along with the Kirat festival of Sakela and the Lepcha festival of Chasok Tangnam.

## Food

Sikkimese food reflects its Nepali, Bhutia, Lepcha and Tibetan communities. **Momos** (steamed dumplings) and noodle dishes such as **thukpa** and thenthuk are everyday favourites. Fermented foods are a speciality, including **gundruk** (fermented greens) and **chhurpi**, a cheese made from yak or cow milk. **Sel roti**, a ring of sweet fried rice batter, is made at festivals.

## Tourist spots

**Gangtok** is the base for most trips. From there, day trips go to **Tsomgo Lake** and **Rumtek Monastery**. North Sikkim, with the **Yumthang Valley** and the high-altitude **Gurudongmar Lake**, is usually visited on an overnight tour via Lachung and Lachen. In the west, **Pelling** is the base for **Pemayangtse Monastery** and for views of Kangchenjunga, while trekkers enter **Khangchendzonga National Park** from Yuksom. The **Buddha Park** at Ravangla lies between Gangtok and Pelling in the south.

## Education and universities

Major institutions include Sikkim University, a central university in Gangtok, the National Institute of Technology Sikkim and Sikkim Manipal University.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Pakyong Airport, about 30 km from Gangtok, opened in 2018 as the state's first airport. Most visitors still fly to **Bagdogra** near Siliguri in West Bengal, about 124 km from Gangtok, and continue by road.

**By rail:** Sikkim has no railway yet. The nearest major stations are New Jalpaiguri and Siliguri in West Bengal. A line from Sevoke to Rangpo in Sikkim is under construction.

**By road:** National Highway 10 links Siliguri with Gangtok along the Teesta valley. Shared jeeps and taxis connect most towns.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Arrange permits early.** Many areas near the borders are restricted, including Tsomgo, Nathu La and North Sikkim, and permits are needed to visit them. Foreign visitors need a permit to enter Sikkim. Tour operators in Gangtok usually arrange these.
- **Allow time to acclimatise.** Gurudongmar and other high places are well above 4,000 m. Ascend gradually and watch for signs of altitude sickness.
- **Expect slow roads.** Mountain roads can close after landslides or snow, especially in the monsoon, so keep spare days in your plan.
- **Travel light on plastic.** Carry a refillable bottle and take your rubbish with you, especially at high lakes and on treks.
