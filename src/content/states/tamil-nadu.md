## Overview

Tamil Nadu is India's southernmost state, on the south-east coast of the peninsula. It had about 72 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 130,060 square kilometres. Its neighbours are Kerala, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh, and it surrounds parts of the union territory of Puducherry. Across the narrow Palk Strait lies Sri Lanka.

The capital and largest city is Chennai. Tamil, the official language, was the first language in India to be recognised as a classical language, with a literature going back more than two thousand years. The state is famous for its temple towns, Bharatanatyam dance and Carnatic music. It has four UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

The region became Madras State after independence, took its present shape in 1956 and was renamed Tamil Nadu ("Tamil country") in 1969.

## History

In the Sangam period, roughly 500 BCE to 300 CE, the Tamil country was ruled by three dynasties: the Cheras in the west, the Pandyas in the south around Madurai, and the Cholas in the Kaveri delta. From the 6th century the Pallavas, based at Kanchipuram, became a major power and patrons of architecture, including the monuments of Mahabalipuram.

The Cholas rose again in the 9th century, with Thanjavur as their capital. Under Rajaraja I and his son Rajendra I in the 11th century, their empire covered southern India and reached across the Indian Ocean. They built the Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur and Gangaikonda Cholapuram. The Pandyas returned to prominence in the 13th century. From the 14th century the Vijayanagara Empire, and later its Nayak governors at Madurai and Thanjavur, ruled the region.

European trading posts appeared in the 17th century, and by the early 19th century the British had brought most of southern India into the Madras Presidency. After independence in 1947 it became Madras State, which then included parts of present-day Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Kerala. The state was reorganised on linguistic lines in 1956 and renamed Tamil Nadu on 14 January 1969.

## Geography and climate

The Western Ghats run along the western border, with the Nilgiri hills and the state's highest peak, Doddabetta (2,636 m). The lower, broken ranges of the Eastern Ghats lie to the north, and a broad coastal plain faces the Bay of Bengal. The Kaveri is the most important river, crossing the state from the Ghats to its delta on the Bay of Bengal. Tamil Nadu has 38 districts and a coastline of about 1,076 km.

Tamil Nadu's rain pattern differs from most of India. About half of its rain comes from the north-east monsoon late in the year, which matters most for the northern and eastern districts, and the rest from the south-west monsoon. The plains are hot for much of the year, while the hill stations of the Nilgiris and Palani Hills stay cool.

## Economy

According to Invest India, Tamil Nadu is India's second-largest state economy, contributing 9.2% of national output, with a per-capita income of ₹2.78 lakh in 2022–23. Its merchandise exports were $52.07 billion in 2024–25.

It is one of India's most industrialised states. It is the country's largest exporter of electronics and leads in exports of engineering goods, ready-made garments, cotton yarn and leather goods. Manufacturing accounts for nearly one-third of the state's economy. The state has four major ports and 17 smaller ones. The Tamil film industry, nicknamed Kollywood, plays an influential role in popular culture.

## Culture, art and festivals

Tamil culture is closely tied to its language, temples and performing arts. **Bharatanatyam**, one of India's oldest classical dance forms, originated here. **Carnatic music** is the classical tradition of the south. Every December and January, Chennai hosts a season of concerts and dance performances.

Temple architecture is the state's most visible heritage. Its towering, sculpted gateway towers (*gopurams*) developed from Pallava and Chola styles. **Pongal**, the four-day harvest festival in mid-January, is the most important celebration of the year.

## Food

Rice is the staple, served with sambar, rasam and vegetable *poriyal*, traditionally on a banana leaf and eaten with the right hand. Breakfast and snack dishes such as **idli**, **dosa**, uthappam, pongal and paniyaram are known across India.

The **Chettinad** region is famous for its spicy, aromatic cooking, and **filter coffee** is the everyday drink.

## Tourist spots

Chennai is the main gateway, with **Mahabalipuram** an easy trip down the coast. The temple circuit takes in **Thanjavur**, Kumbakonam, Tiruchirappalli, **Madurai** and **Rameswaram**, with **Kanyakumari** at the tip of the peninsula. In the west, the Nilgiris offer **Ooty**, the mountain railway and **Mudumalai**, and the Palani Hills have **Kodaikanal**. **Velankanni** on the coast is one of India's most important Christian pilgrimage sites.

## Education and universities

Chennai is a major centre for higher education, with IIT Madras, Anna University and the University of Madras. NIT Tiruchirappalli is one of India's leading engineering institutes, and Tamil Nadu Agricultural University is based in Coimbatore.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Chennai International Airport is the main gateway, with flights across India and abroad. Coimbatore, Madurai and Tiruchirappalli also have airports, and Coimbatore is the nearest for Ooty and the Nilgiris.

**By rail:** Tamil Nadu is served by the Southern Railway, and Chennai has trains to all parts of India. Main lines run south to Madurai, Rameswaram and Kanyakumari and west to Coimbatore. The Nilgiri Mountain Railway climbs from Mettupalayam to Ooty.

**By road:** National highways link Chennai with Bengaluru, Tiruchirappalli, Madurai and Kanyakumari. Government and private buses serve almost every town.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Dress respectfully at temples.** Cover shoulders and knees and remove shoes. Some inner shrines are open only to Hindus.
- **Check temple timings.** Many large temples close for a few hours in the middle of the day.
- **Book the Nilgiri train early.** Seats on the mountain railway are limited and sell out in the holiday season.
- **Watch for late-year rain.** October to December brings the north-east monsoon, and cyclones occasionally affect the coast.
