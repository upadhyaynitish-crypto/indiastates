## Overview

Telangana is a landlocked state on the Deccan Plateau in southern India. It borders Maharashtra and Chhattisgarh to the north, Karnataka to the west and Andhra Pradesh to the south and east. It is India's newest state, formed on 2 June 2014 from the north-western part of Andhra Pradesh. It had about 35 million people at the 2011 Census, covers 112,077 square kilometres and has 33 districts.

The capital, Hyderabad, is one of India's largest cities, a centre of the technology and pharmaceutical industries, and famous for its Indo-Islamic monuments and its food. Telugu is the main official language, and Urdu is the second official language.

Outside Hyderabad, the old Kakatiya heartland around Warangal has some of the finest medieval temples in the Deccan, including the Ramappa Temple, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## History

The region was part of the Satavahana realm from about 230 BCE to 220 CE, and later of the Chalukya and Rashtrakuta empires. Its golden age came under the **Kakatiya dynasty**, which ruled from Warangal between 1083 and 1323 and built the Ramappa and Thousand Pillar temples.

In the 14th century the area came under the Delhi Sultanate and then the Bahmani Sultanate. In 1518 the governor of Golconda founded the **Qutb Shahi dynasty**, which built Hyderabad and the Charminar. The Mughal emperor Aurangzeb took Golconda in 1687 after a long siege. In 1724 the Mughal viceroy of the Deccan established the **Asaf Jahi dynasty**, whose rulers, the Nizams of Hyderabad, governed the princely state of Hyderabad until 1948.

When India became independent in 1947, the Nizam wished to remain independent. In September 1948, after an Indian military operation known as Operation Polo, Hyderabad State became part of India. In 1956 its Telugu-speaking districts were merged with Andhra State to form Andhra Pradesh. A long-running movement for a separate state led to the creation of Telangana on 2 June 2014.

## Geography and climate

Telangana lies on the Deccan Plateau and is drained by two of India's great rivers, the Godavari in the north and the Krishna in the south. Even so, much of the land is dry, and irrigation projects such as the Nagarjuna Sagar Dam are vital to farming.

The climate is semi-arid, hot and dry for most of the year. The monsoon arrives in June and lasts until late September. Annual rainfall is about 900 to 1,500 mm in the north and 700 to 900 mm in the south.

## Economy

Telangana has one of India's larger state economies, driven by services and industry around Hyderabad. According to Invest India, the state is the country's largest vaccine and pharmaceutical hub, accounting for about 40% of India's drug production, and Genome Valley is India's largest life-sciences cluster. Hyderabad also has the country's second-largest IT corridor.

Other important industries include automobiles and components, textiles, minerals and food processing. Telangana is India's third-largest cotton producer. Hyderabad is home to several national defence and aerospace research laboratories, and to the Telugu film industry.

## Culture, art and festivals

Telangana's culture blends Telugu traditions with the Persian and Urdu-influenced court culture of Hyderabad. Folk and performing arts include *Burra katha* storytelling, shadow puppetry (*Tholu bommalata*), the Gussadi dance and the vigorous *Perini Shivatandavam*.

The two great state festivals are **Bathukamma**, a flower festival celebrated by women in the autumn, and **Bonalu**, in honour of the goddess Mahakali. The biennial **Sammakka Saralamma Jatara** at Medaram is one of the largest tribal gatherings in India.

## Food

Hyderabadi cooking is famous across India. **Hyderabadi biryani**, **haleem** and kebabs come from the city's royal and Muslim kitchens. In the countryside, millet rotis made from jowar and bajra, lentils and spicy curries are everyday food.

## Tourist spots

Most visitors start in **Hyderabad**: the Charminar and its bazaars, Chowmahalla Palace, Golconda Fort, the Qutb Shahi Tombs and Hussain Sagar lake. To the north-east, **Warangal** and Hanamkonda have Warangal Fort and the Thousand Pillar Temple, and the **Ramappa Temple** lies further on at Palampet.

## Education and universities

Hyderabad is one of India's major education and research centres. Its institutions include Osmania University, the University of Hyderabad, IIT Hyderabad and IIIT Hyderabad. NIT Warangal is a leading engineering institute.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Rajiv Gandhi International Airport at Shamshabad, south of Hyderabad, is the state's only major airport and has flights across India and abroad.

**By rail:** Secunderabad is the headquarters of the South Central Railway. Hyderabad's stations have direct trains to Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru and Kolkata.

**By road:** National highways connect Hyderabad with Bengaluru, Mumbai, Nagpur and Vijayawada.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Avoid the peak of summer.** March to May is very hot on the Deccan Plateau.
- **Dress modestly in the old city and at religious sites.** Remove shoes at mosques, dargahs and temples, and check whether non-worshippers may enter.
- **Climb Golconda early or late.** It's a long, exposed walk to the top, best done in the cooler hours.
- **Combine Warangal and Ramappa.** The temples and fort are a comfortable overnight trip from Hyderabad.
