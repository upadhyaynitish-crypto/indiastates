## Overview

Uttar Pradesh, in the north of India, is the country's most populous state. Nearly 200 million people lived here at the 2011 Census, more than in most countries. It covers 240,928 square kilometres of the Ganges plain and borders nine states and territories, more than any other state, as well as Nepal to the north.

The capital is Lucknow. The state holds some of the best-known sights in India: the Taj Mahal at Agra, the riverfront of Varanasi, and Sarnath, where the Buddha gave his first sermon. It is central to several faiths. Varanasi, Ayodhya, Mathura and Prayagraj are among Hinduism's most important pilgrimage places. Sarnath and Kushinagar are sacred to Buddhists, and the state has been a centre of Islamic culture and learning since medieval times.

The state took the name Uttar Pradesh ("northern province") on 24 January 1950. Hindi is the official language, and Urdu is an additional official language.

## History

The plains of the Ganges and Yamuna were the heartland of many of India's great empires. The ancient kingdom of Kosala, with its capital at Ayodhya, lay here, and the region was central to the Maurya (c. 320–200 BCE) and Gupta (c. 350–600 CE) empires. In the 7th century, Harshavardhana ruled much of northern India from Kannauj.

The Delhi Sultanate controlled the region from 1206 to 1526, a period that also saw the spread of Sufism. Under the Mughals it became the centre of the empire. Akbar built Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri, and Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal. As Mughal power declined in the 18th century, the Nawabs of Awadh made Lucknow a centre of art, cuisine and architecture, and the Marathas extended their influence into the region.

The British East India Company took control in stages. The region was a main theatre of the Indian Rebellion of 1857. It was later organised as the United Provinces of Agra and Oudh, usually shortened to "UP". After independence the United Provinces were renamed Uttar Pradesh, with effect from 24 January 1950. In 2000 the northern hill districts became the separate state of Uttarakhand.

## Geography and climate

Most of the state lies on the fertile **Gangetic plain**, which is intensively farmed. The Ganges and its tributary the Yamuna meet at the Triveni Sangam in Prayagraj. Other major rivers include the Ghaghara, Gomti, Sarayu and Betwa. Along the northern border runs the **Terai**, a belt of tall grassland, forest and marsh. In the south are the hills and plateau of the **Vindhya** range and Bundelkhand.

Cultural regions run from **Braj** and Rohilkhand in the west, through **Awadh** and Bundelkhand in the centre, to **Purvanchal** in the east. The state has 75 districts.

Winters (January and February) are cool, summers (March to May) are very hot, and most rain falls in the monsoon from June to September.

## Economy

Uttar Pradesh has India's third-largest state economy, according to Invest India. Agriculture is a mainstay. The state is the country's largest producer of sugar, and it grew about 19% of India's food grains in 2014–15. Services now make up the largest share of the economy.

Invest India lists textiles, electronics, IT services, leather and footwear and renewable energy as focus sectors, and notes the state's network of expressways and its large number of operational airports. Exports were $21.98 billion in 2024–25.

## Culture, art and festivals

Uttar Pradesh is the home of **Kathak**, one of India's classical dance forms, with famous schools (*gharanas*) in Lucknow and Varanasi. Varanasi is also a centre of Hindustani classical music. **Ramlila**, the dramatic retelling of the life of Rama, is one of the oldest folk theatre traditions and is performed across the state before Dussehra.

The **Kumbh Mela** at Prayagraj, held every twelve years, is one of the largest gatherings of people in the world. Holi in the Braj region around Mathura and Vrindavan, Diwali and Eid are all major occasions.

## Food

The cooking of **Awadh**, centred on Lucknow, is famous for its kebabs, biryani and slow-cooked dishes. Varanasi and other cities are known for vegetarian food and street snacks such as **chaat** and **kachori**. Agra is known for **petha**, and Mathura for its **peda**. Wheat breads, lentils and seasonal vegetables are the everyday staples.

## Tourist spots

**Agra** has the Taj Mahal and Agra Fort, with **Fatehpur Sikri** close by, and is often combined with **Mathura** and **Vrindavan**. **Lucknow** has the Bara Imambara and the architecture of the Nawabs. In the east, **Varanasi** and **Sarnath** are usually visited together. **Prayagraj** and **Ayodhya** are within reach, and Buddhist pilgrims continue to Kushinagar and Shravasti. **Dudhwa National Park** lies in the Terai near the Nepal border.

## Education and universities

The state has some of India's best-known universities, including Banaras Hindu University, Aligarh Muslim University and the University of Allahabad. IIT Kanpur and IIM Lucknow are leading national institutes. Invest India ranks the state first in India for the number of colleges.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Lucknow and Varanasi have the main international airports. Agra, Prayagraj, Ayodhya, Gorakhpur and Kushinagar also have airports. Phase I of Noida International Airport, near Delhi, was inaugurated on 28 March 2026. Many visitors to Agra fly into Delhi.

**By rail:** The state has an extensive rail network. Prayagraj is the headquarters of the North Central Railway and Gorakhpur of the North Eastern Railway. Agra, Lucknow, Kanpur and Varanasi are major stations with fast trains to Delhi.

**By road:** Expressways link the major cities, including the Yamuna Expressway towards Agra and the Agra–Lucknow Expressway.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Check the Taj Mahal's closing day.** It is closed to visitors on Fridays. Go at sunrise for the best light and smaller crowds.
- **Allow for winter fog.** In December and January, fog can delay trains and flights across the plains.
- **Dress modestly at religious sites.** Remove shoes at temples, mosques and imambaras, and ask before taking photographs. Do not photograph the cremation ghats in Varanasi.
- **Book early for festival periods.** Rooms and trains fill up well in advance for the Kumbh Mela, Holi in Braj and Diwali.
