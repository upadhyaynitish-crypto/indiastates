## Overview

Uttarakhand is a mountain state in north India, on the southern slopes of the Himalayas. It borders Himachal Pradesh to the north-west and Uttar Pradesh to the south, with international borders with the Tibet Autonomous Region of China to the north and Nepal to the east. It had about 10.1 million people at the 2011 Census and covers 53,483 square kilometres.

**Dehradun** is the capital (the winter capital). Bhararisain in Gairsain is the summer capital, and Nainital is the seat of the High Court. The state has two regions, **Garhwal** in the west and **Kumaon** in the east. Hindi is the official language and Sanskrit a second official language, while Garhwali and Kumaoni are widely spoken.

Two of India's great rivers begin here: the **Ganges** at the Gangotri glacier and the **Yamuna** at Yamunotri. Because of its many temples and pilgrim routes, the state is often called *Devabhumi*, the "land of the gods".

## History

Stone Age rock shelters have been found in Kumaon, and an edict of Ashoka at Kalsi shows that Buddhism reached the region early. The Katyuri dynasty dominated much of the area between about the 4th and 14th centuries. Later, the **Garhwal Kingdom** ruled the west and the **Kumaon Kingdom** the east, and the Pahari school of painting developed.

In 1791 the expanding Gorkha kingdom of Nepal took Almora, and in 1803 Garhwal. After the Anglo-Nepalese War, the region passed to the British under the Treaty of Sugauli, while a smaller Garhwal princely state survived at Tehri. After independence, the hills became part of Uttar Pradesh.

The 1970s saw the **Chipko movement**, a protest against commercial logging led largely by village women. A long campaign for a separate hill state ended when Uttarakhand became the 27th state of India on **9 November 2000**. It was called Uttaranchal until 2007.

## Geography and climate

More than 86% of Uttarakhand is mountainous, according to Invest India. It rises from the Terai plains at about 300 m to **Nanda Devi**, at 7,816 m the state's highest peak. About 45% of the land is forest. Snow-fed rivers such as the Mandakini, which flows past Kedarnath, cut deep valleys through Garhwal.

The plains and foothills are hot in summer and mild in winter, the middle hills are temperate, and the high mountains have freezing winters and heavy snow. The monsoon, from June to September, brings most of the rain and frequent landslides.

## Economy

Tourism and pilgrimage are central to the economy, and Invest India calls Rishikesh the "Yoga Capital of the World". The industrial belt in the plains is important too: Invest India says the state makes 22% of India's generic medicines and is the second-largest maker of two-wheelers. Hydropower, horticulture and hill farming are other mainstays, with hardy crops such as buckwheat and finger millet grown in the interior.

## Culture, art and festivals

Garhwali and Kumaoni traditions shape the culture of the hills. Crafts include *likhai* wood carving on temple and house doors, and **aipan**, a ritual Kumaoni floor and wall art that has a Geographical Indication tag.

Haridwar is one of the four places that host the **Kumbh Mela**, and both the Kumbh Mela and **Ramman**, a ritual masked theatre from Chamoli, are on UNESCO's list of intangible heritage. The Kumaoni festival of **Harela** marks the rainy season, and every 12 years the **Nanda Devi Raj Jat** pilgrimage crosses the high Himalayas of Chamoli.

## Food

The hill food of Uttarakhand is simple and nourishing, built on coarse grains such as finger millet (**mandua**), buckwheat and barnyard millet, cooked in ghee or mustard oil. Popular dishes include **bhatt ki dal**, made from black soybeans, and **jhoi**, a local kadhi. **Bal mithai**, a fudge-like sweet from Kumaon, is the best known.

## Tourist spots

**Rishikesh** and **Haridwar**, on the Ganges at the edge of the hills, are the main gateways. **Jim Corbett National Park**, India's oldest, is in the Kumaon foothills, and **Nainital** and **Mussoorie** are classic hill stations. High in Garhwal, the **Char Dham** pilgrimage visits Yamunotri, Gangotri, **Kedarnath** and Badrinath, and the alpine **Valley of Flowers** is at its best in the monsoon.

## Education and universities

Major institutions include IIT Roorkee, IIM Kashipur, AIIMS Rishikesh, Hemvati Nandan Bahuguna Garhwal University and the Forest Research Institute in Dehradun.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Dehradun Airport at Jolly Grant, about 20 km from Rishikesh and 35 km from Haridwar, is the main airport. Pantnagar Airport serves Kumaon and Nainital.

**By rail:** Railways are limited to the plains. Haridwar and Dehradun are on lines from Delhi and Howrah, and Kathgodam is the railhead for Nainital.

**By road:** Roads link Delhi with Haridwar, Rishikesh and Dehradun and continue into the mountains. Hill roads are slow and can close after heavy rain.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Check the temple and trek opening dates.** Kedarnath, Badrinath and the Valley of Flowers open only in the warmer months, on dates announced each year.
- **Avoid the high roads in the monsoon.** July and August bring landslides that can strand travellers in the mountains.
- **Check Char Dham rules before you go.** Find out from the official tourism site whether pilgrims need to register in advance that season.
- **Respect local customs in pilgrim towns.** Rishikesh is a vegetarian-only, alcohol-free town.
