## Overview

West Bengal stretches from the Himalayas in the north to the Bay of Bengal in the south. It borders five Indian states (Sikkim, Assam, Bihar, Jharkhand and Odisha) and three countries: Bangladesh to the east, and Nepal and Bhutan to the north. It had about 91 million people at the 2011 Census in an area of 88,752 square kilometres, the second-highest population density of any state.

The capital, Kolkata (formerly Calcutta), was the capital of British India until 1911 and remains one of the country's great cultural cities. The state takes in the tea-covered hills of Darjeeling, the plains of the Ganges delta and the mangrove forests of the Sundarbans. It has three UNESCO World Heritage Sites, and Kolkata's Durga Puja is on UNESCO's list of intangible cultural heritage.

Bengali and English are the official languages, and Nepali has official status in the Darjeeling hills.

## History

Bengal was part of the Mauryan and Gupta empires. From the 8th century the Buddhist Pala dynasty ruled the region for about four hundred years, followed by the Sena dynasty. The Bengal Sultanate, founded in 1352, made the region a major trading power. It was absorbed into the Mughal Empire in 1576. In the early 18th century Bengal became semi-independent under the Nawabs, who ruled from Murshidabad.

In 1757 the East India Company defeated Nawab Siraj ud-Daulah at the Battle of Plassey, and Bengal became the base of British power in India. Calcutta served as the capital of British India until 1911. The 19th century brought the **Bengal Renaissance**, a period of social reform, education and creative achievement associated with figures such as Rabindranath Tagore, who won the Nobel Prize in Literature.

When India became independent in 1947, Bengal was partitioned. The western part became the Indian state of West Bengal, and the eastern part became East Bengal in Pakistan, later the independent country of Bangladesh. Partition and the 1971 war brought millions of refugees into the state. The princely state of Cooch Behar joined West Bengal in 1950.

## Geography and climate

West Bengal is long and narrow, with very different landscapes from north to south. The **Darjeeling Himalaya** in the far north includes Sandakphu (3,636 m), the state's highest peak. Below the hills lie the Terai and the forests and tea gardens of the Dooars, then the plains of North Bengal. Further south are the **Ganges delta** and, to the west, the drier uplands of the **Rarh** region. On the coast, the **Sundarbans** are part of the world's largest mangrove forest.

The Ganges divides as it enters the state. One branch flows into Bangladesh as the Padma, and the other runs south past Kolkata as the Bhagirathi and Hooghly. The Teesta, Torsa and Mahananda are the main rivers of the north. The state has 23 districts.

The monsoon brings rain to the whole state from June to September, and storms from the Bay of Bengal sometimes affect the coast. Summers are hot and humid on the plains, while Darjeeling stays cool all year.

## Economy

West Bengal's economy combines agriculture, industry and services. Rice is the main food crop, and potatoes, jute, sugarcane and wheat are other major crops. It is India's second-largest producer of tea, according to Invest India, and its Darjeeling teas are known around the world. The state accounts for about half of India's leather goods exports.

Kolkata is the state's main commercial centre, served by the ports of Kolkata and Haldia. Invest India lists agriculture and food, textiles, IT services and leather among the state's focus sectors.

## Culture, art and festivals

Bengal has a celebrated literary and artistic tradition. **Rabindranath Tagore** shaped modern Bengali literature and music, and his songs (*Rabindra Sangeet*) are still sung everywhere. The **Bauls**, wandering mystic singers, represent an older folk tradition. Bengali cinema is known for art films, including the work of the director Satyajit Ray.

**Durga Puja**, held in September or October, is the high point of the year, when Kolkata and other towns fill with elaborate temporary shrines and crowds stay out through the night. Other important occasions include Poila Boishakh (the Bengali New Year), Kali Puja, Eid and Christmas. Crafts include the terracotta art of Bishnupur and Baluchari silk saris.

## Food

Bengali food centres on rice and fish, especially freshwater fish. **Machher jhol** (fish curry) is the everyday dish, and hilsa (*ilish*) is the most prized fish. The cuisine is equally famous for its sweets, made from fresh cheese (*chhena*): **rosogolla**, **sandesh** and **mishti doi**. Kolkata's street food includes **kathi rolls** and phuchka, and **Darjeeling tea** is the drink of the hills.

## Tourist spots

**Kolkata** has the Victoria Memorial, Howrah Bridge, the riverside temples of Dakshineswar and Belur Math, and a rich legacy of colonial-era buildings. In the north, **Darjeeling** offers tea estates, mountain views and the famous hill railway. In the south, the **Sundarbans** are explored by boat. Inland, **Santiniketan**, the terracotta temples of **Bishnupur** and the Nawabs' palaces at **Murshidabad** each make a rewarding trip.

## Education and universities

Kolkata has long been a centre of learning. The University of Calcutta, Jadavpur University and Presidency University are based there, along with IIM Calcutta. IIT Kharagpur was the first of the Indian Institutes of Technology, and Visva-Bharati at Santiniketan was founded by Rabindranath Tagore.

## Getting there (air, rail, road)

**By air:** Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport in Kolkata is the main gateway to eastern India. Bagdogra airport, near Siliguri, serves Darjeeling and the north.

**By rail:** Kolkata's Howrah and Sealdah stations are among the busiest in India, with trains to every part of the country. New Jalpaiguri is the main station for the north and the starting point of the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway.

**By road:** National highways link Kolkata with Odisha, Jharkhand, Bihar and the north of the state. Within Kolkata, the metro, buses and ferries help with getting around.

## Quick tips for visitors

- **Book early for Durga Puja.** Hotels and transport fill up weeks ahead, and Kolkata's streets are very crowded during the festival.
- **Reserve the toy train in advance.** Seats on the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway are limited.
- **Visit the Sundarbans with a registered operator.** Entry needs a permit, and trips are by boat with a guide.
- **Carry rain gear in the monsoon.** June to September brings heavy rain and occasional cyclones on the coast.
